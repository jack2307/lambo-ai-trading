Slice 1 of the 2026-09-23 three-month search: how it was run and how to read it
==============================================================================

Registration: docs/hypotheses/2026-09-23-three-month-search.md, committed at
0115da6 before this run existed. Nothing below softens its falsifier or its
four reporting commitments.

The slice is five mechanisms and their full registry grids: ema-cross,
rsi-reversion, donchian-breakout, bb-fade and keltner-break. Each grid is
three values on two axes, so the slice is 45 cells and every one of them was
run. The receipt beside this file, slice-1-xauusd-15m.txt, prints all 45 rows
in grid order before it ranks them, so the best of 45 can be read against the
44 it beat rather than quoted on its own.

A cell here is a strategy and one fully specified point of its grid. It is run
through the existing walk-forward at the configured four folds with every grid
axis pinned by a Preset, which is what makes "same strategy, same parameters,
no re-fit" checkable: with the axes pinned the grid a fold could sweep is
empty, so each fold selects the one cell it was given and then measures it on
the window that follows. The in-sample figure for a cell is therefore the
combined return over the four measured folds, not over the whole window, and
the first fifth of the window is training only. The spread is the configured
0.28, the guards are the market's own and were on, the gate is the registry's
min_trades 30 / min_profit_factor 1.2 / min_expectancy_r 0.05, and none of the
three was touched.

The nine-month leg is the replay, not a second search. It uses the fixed path
that runs the cell at those same parameters over the whole window with no
folds and no selection at all, which is the harsher of the two available
readings and the one the registration's wording requires. Each cell is read
against 200 count-matched control runs, with the count matching repaired
earlier today; the achieved match is printed on every row so a percentile read
against a null of the wrong size can be spotted instead of trusted.

The windows were cut with the end dates inclusive, so the two do not overlap
by a day and do not skip one: in sample is 2026-06-23 through 2026-09-23 and
out of sample is 2025-09-23 through 2026-06-22. The gold feed on disk stops at
2026-09-17 13:00, so the in-sample window is 5731 bars and ends there rather
than on the 23rd. That is the data, not a choice, and it shortens the search
window by six days.

Two deviations are recorded rather than argued. The registration said the top
five cells overall would be carried forward; the search was split across four
agents by mechanism, so twenty cells are carried in total and five of them are
this slice's. That is more out-of-sample tests than the registration promised,
not fewer, so it cannot flatter the result. Second, the driver that runs this
protocol is a new binary, crates/fd-backtest/src/bin/slice_three_month.rs. It
sets no threshold and computes no metric of its own; it enumerates the grids,
calls the existing hypothesis paths, and prints what they return, including
the return over the window that the standing hypotheses receipt format does
not have a column for.

How to read the outcome. On this slice the search did not clear 50% even in
sample: the best cell returned 12.90% over the three months, which is a
quarter of the target and was produced by a search over 45 cells on 5731 bars.
Of the five cells carried forward, none survived the nine months. Two failed
all three legs outright, and the three keltner-break cells kept a positive
expectancy there while failing the 1.2 profit factor and sitting at the 69th,
78th and 79th percentile of their own matched nulls rather than the 95th. The
first row's null is 0.73 of the method's trade count, outside the quarter-wide
band the repair set, so that percentile is not that method's percentile; the
row fails on expectancy and profit factor without it, so nothing rests on the
unmatched figure.

Nothing here goes on the funded account, and the registration forbids it
doing so whatever it showed.
