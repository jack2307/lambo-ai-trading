# Entering BEFORE a scheduled release — the branch `agent/n1` made writable and did not measure

**Date:** 2026-10-07 (pre-registration; written and committed before the first
line of code)
**Question:** `Filter::NewsOnly` takes **signed** offsets, so `newsonly:-30/0`
can finally say "only enter in the 30 minutes *before* a release". Does that
window contain a directional edge — and is any edge it shows a property of the
entry rule, or only of being exposed when the release lands?
**Outcome:** open. This file is the registration, not the result.

## 0. Why this branch exists and has never been measured

`docs/decisions/2026-10-06-news-entry.md` §12 names it and declines it:
"Nothing here measures entering **before** a release." That registration built
the machinery (`news::window_in` with signed offsets, `Filter::NewsOnly`) and
spent its cells on the post-release side. The pre-release side is mechanically
different: after a release the information is public and the question is
whether the move persists; before one, no information has arrived, and the only
things that can carry an edge are **positioning** and **liquidity**.

Measured basis from n1 §2, **independently reproduced here** before anything
was declared (504 of 517 USD impact-3 events in the bars' own span land exactly
on a 15m bar open; numbers identical to three decimals):

| offset (min) | −120 | −90 | −60 | −45 | −30 | −15 | **0** | +15 | +45 | +60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| median 15m range / day median | 0.92 | 1.02 | 1.06 | 1.11 | 1.33 | **1.78** | **5.96** | 2.92 | 2.40 | 2.58 |

The pre-release ramp is real: 1.33× at −30 and 1.78× at −15.

## 1. Hypotheses — two mechanisms, measured separately, plus a control

- **Pre-news breakout (`mode = 0`).** Positioning ahead of a scheduled event is
  directional, so the drift in the final half hour continues for the rest of
  that half hour. Enter with it, be **flat at the release**.
- **Pre-news volatility compression (`mode = 1`).** The range contracts ahead of
  the print and then expands; enter whichever way it leaves the contracted band
  first, be **flat at the release**.
- **Hold through the release (`holdThrough = 1`), the CONTROL.** The same
  entries as the breakout branch, held deliberately **into** the 5.96× bar.

Prediction, stated so it can come back no: **all three pass 0 cells.** The
pre-release ramp is thinning liquidity, not accumulating drift, and the control
converts a known variance event into a coin flip at a cost of one spread.

**The control is the point of the job, and its reading is fixed in advance:**
branch 3 shares its entry bar, side and stop with branch 1 and differs *only*
in whether the position is still open when the release prints. So **if branch 3
passes while branches 1 and 2 fail, what has been measured is exposure to the
event, not a mechanism for entering before it** — and that sentence is the
result, not an interpretation of it.

### (e) Event scope of the flat-at-release exit

The exit anchors on the next event of impact ≥ 3 of **any** currency, because a
`Strategy` cannot read `TradingRules` (n1's note). Counted: **2 of 562** USD
impact-3 releases have a non-USD impact-3 event inside the 30 or 45 minutes
before them, and **0 of 737** impact-3 events share a timestamp with another.
The ambiguity touches 0.4% of events and its direction is conservative — it can
only flatten *earlier*, never hold into a release.

## 3. What is being built

1. `news::next_event_at_or_after` — the mirror of `last_event_at_or_before`,
   with the same `None`-when-no-calendar semantics. Nothing existing changes.
2. Strategy **`news-pre`** (a new id; none of the 20 already-measured
   mechanisms is touched).

`Filter::NewsOnly` already takes signed offsets and is **not** modified. Its
spelling is verified, not assumed: `from_min: i32, to_min: i32`, parsed with
`parse::<i32>()`, `/` as separator so `newsonly:-30/-15` is unambiguous.

**The one design fact the branch depends on, verified in the source before it
was relied on:** `Filtered::on_bar` gates only `Intent::Enter`
(`if !matches!(intent, Intent::Enter { .. }) { return intent; }`). An
`Intent::Exit` passes the filter untouched, so a position opened inside
`newsonly:-30/-15` can still be closed on the release bar, which lies outside
that gate. Without this the flat-at-release branches could not exist.

## 4. The rule, frozen

Signal bar `i` is whatever the row's `newsonly:` gate admits. With no position:

- **`mode = 0`, pre-news breakout.**
  `preMove = bars[i].close − bars[i − preBars + 1].open`.
  Gate: `|preMove| ≥ minMoveAtr × ATR(atrPeriod)` read at `i − preBars`, i.e.
  **before the span being measured**, so the ruler is not stretched by the move
  it is measuring (n1's discipline, kept).
  **Side** = sign of `preMove`.
  **Stop** = `stopImpulse ×` (`max high − min low` over `bars[i−preBars+1..=i]`)
  beyond entry on the losing side — the move's own range, so a failure is a
  return through it.
- **`mode = 1`, compression break.**
  Quiet window = `bars[i−quietBars .. i]` (ending at the bar *before* the
  signal bar). `squeeze = (max high − min low) / (quietBars × ATR(atrPeriod))`
  with ATR read at `i − quietBars − 1`.
  Gate: `squeeze < maxSqueeze` **and** `bars[i].close` outside the quiet band.
  **Side** = the side it closed outside on.
  **Stop** = `stopImpulse ×` the quiet band's own height beyond entry.
- **Exit.** `holdThrough = 0`: on every bar with a position, let `e` be the next
  impact-≥`minImpact` event strictly after `bars[i].time`; if `bars[i+1].time ≥ e`
  (or there is no next bar), emit `Intent::Exit`. The engine fills at
  `bars[i+1].open`, which **is** the release bar's open, and engine step 1 runs
  before step 2, so the release bar's range never touches the position. This
  reads the next bar's **timestamp** only — never its prices — and that is the
  same clock the engine itself uses to place a fill.
  `holdThrough = 1`: `Intent::None`; the engine's stop, 1.8R target and 4-hour
  cap govern, and the position is open when the release prints.
- The fill is the next bar's open and `Exits::Engine` applies throughout, so
  branch 1 and branch 3 are identical in entry bar, side, stop and sizing.
  One entry per release: a position is open, or the gate has closed.

Frozen: `minImpact = 3`, `atrPeriod = 14`, `stopImpulse = 1.0`,
`reward_risk = 1.8` and `max_hold_ms = 4 h` (config), `spread = 0.28`
(`markets.xauduka`), `swap_long/short_per_lot = 0.0` — so §4(i)'s
expectancy-vs-PF swap blindness **cannot** bite this job, and that is checked
rather than assumed. `grid()` is empty; every row is a separately declared
rule and runs use `--fixed`.

## 5. The null enters in the same window

The matched null in `hypotheses.rs` is `RandomEntry` wrapped in **the row's own
filters**, so a `newsonly:-30/-15` row's control draws from the same single
pre-release bar of the same releases. What stays different is the gate and the
direction, which is the claim.

**§4(v) is binding and is the reason the windows are what they are.**
`matched_rate` probes once at `entryRate = 0.02` and scales linearly, so on a
narrow gate the probe draws 0–1 trades and the scaling has no purchase (n1
measured count match down to **0.04** on a one-year window). On four-year
windows n1 measured 0.82–1.52. Every row's achieved `null trades` median is read
against `COUNT_MATCH_BAND = 0.25`; **any row outside the band is reported
unmatched and its percentile withheld**, with the gate alone doing the work.
`matched_rate` is **not** touched. `--null-sides=coin`, the default; any row
printing a long share outside 0.40–0.60 is re-run with `--null-sides=exposure`
and those cells are added to the ledger.

## 10. Falsifier — specific, and it can fire

1. **Primary.** If all three branches pass **0 cells** on **both** windows in
   **both** arms, the pre-release phase of a scheduled event contains no
   mechanism reachable by 15m bars. Combined with n1's result — the only
   survivor sits at **+45 minutes**, after the print — the desk then knows that
   the one place in a scheduled event's life cycle worth looking at is **after
   the release, once the spread has come back**, and that the half hour before
   it is not worth code.
2. **Exposure, not mechanism.** If branch 3 passes while branches 1 and 2 fail,
   the measured thing is **exposure to the event**, because branch 3 differs
   from branch 1 only in whether the position is open when the release prints.
   Declared now so it cannot be reported as an entry edge later.
3. **Cost.** If a cell passes at 0.28 but breaks below the spread actually paid
   in −30..0 (measured §2(a): p50 0.220, p90 0.220, max 0.260), the pass is an
   artefact of cost. Unlike n1's case the measured spread is *below* config, so
   this fires only if the break point is under ~0.26.
4. **Zero cost.** If nothing passes at `--spread=0.00`, no execution improvement
   can rescue the branch.
5. **Instrument.** Count match outside `COUNT_MATCH_BAND` on most rows →
   percentiles withheld. A branch-1/2 row whose `SIGNAL` exit does **not**
   dominate, or a branch-3 row that is not open at the release, means the rule
   did not do what it was written to do and the row is void, not a result.
   Any non-zero `wrong_side_stop` voids its row.

Already fired at pre-check, before any gate cell was spent (brief §6):
**(b)** the compression premise — the −30..0 window is 1.56× the same-day
median span, and no strict spelling reaches 40 trades on both windows; and
**(d)** `minMoveAtr = 1.5` — 6 to 31 trades against a floor of 40.

## 12. What this will not say

- Nothing here measures 1m or 5m bars. The pre-release ramp inside a 15m bar
  may be a tradable sequence of 1m bars; this registration cannot say.
- Nothing here measures limit entries, which is how a desk would really place a
  pre-positioning trade, nor the slippage of a market order near a print.
- The spread evidence is **one FOMC** at one read per 20 seconds. It establishes
  the sustained pre-release spread and nothing about a worst tick.
- `events.parquet` is 747 events dominated by NFP (203), CPI (203) and FOMC
  (143+). "High-impact USD news" means those three.
  `events-extended.parquet` exists on disk and is **not** used; the `news:` line
  of every receipt is quoted to prove which calendar was installed.
- Only gold. A pre-release effect is a positioning claim and positioning differs
  by instrument; one market cannot close the question for the others.

## What would reopen this

A 1m or 5m measurement of the same half hour; a limit-entry fill model; a
per-minute spread series across many releases rather than one FOMC; the same
rows on `eurduka` (whose calendar scope would be EUR releases); or a relaxation
of `news_flat_before_min`, which is currently a flat prohibition on the whole
branch.

### Outcome

**Falsifier 1 fires.** All three branches pass 0 cells on both windows in both
arms, every row at adequate sample size, and nothing reaches even break-even at
zero cost. The half hour before a scheduled release **contains no mechanism
reachable by 15m bars** — and the pre-release drift rule is measurably worse
than random entry in the same minutes.

Set beside n1's result, whose only survivor sits at **+45 minutes** after the
print, once the spread has come back: the one place in a scheduled event's life
cycle that has repaid a look is **after the release, not before it**. The
pre-release ramp in the range (1.33× at −30, 1.78× at −15) is variance without
drift — the median pre-release displacement is only 0.51–0.74 ATR while the
range runs 1.33–1.78× normal — and a rule cannot trade variance it has to cross
a spread to reach.

Three things this does **not** say: it is one instrument; it is 15m bars only;
and it is a market-order fill model. §12 stands.
