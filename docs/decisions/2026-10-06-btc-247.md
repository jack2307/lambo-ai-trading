# BTC 24/7 — pre-registration (axis m7)

**Date:** 2026-10-06 (registration; written and committed BEFORE the first run)
**Question:** on a tape with no weekend gap and no session close, does the
`gold-intraday` mechanism set clear the desk's gate on both windows — and is
BTC's per-round cost, in R, already larger than the edge the gate demands?
**Outcome:** PENDING at registration.

## Hypothesis

One sentence: BTC removes the one variable every gold measurement must live
with — the break — so if "crypto 24/7 is easier" is true, the same thirteen
`gold-intraday` constructs should clear `PF >= 1.200` and
`expectancy >= +0.050R` on BTC where they do not on gold; the competing claim
is that BTC's spread, expressed as `spread / stop`, is on its own larger than
+0.050R and therefore closes the question by arithmetic before any PF is read.

## Instruments — named, because they are not the same thing

- `--market=btc`: `BTCUSDT-15m.parquet`, Binance spot tape, `contract_size 1.0
  BTC`, configured `spread = 5.00` USD of price.
- `--market=btcusd`: `BTCUSD-15m.parquet`, Vantage `BTCUSD.sc` CFD,
  `contract_size 0.01`, configured `spread = 17.05` USD of price (MEASURED,
  3.4x the Binance assumption).

Both files exist in `/e/rust/flowdesk/data/bars/` and both cover both windows
(checked before registration: BTCUSDT-15m 70,080 bars 2024-09-12 → 2026-09-12;
BTCUSD-15m 100,798 bars 2023-10-05 → 2026-09-15). Every figure reported will
say which of the two it came from.

## Falsifier — and it can fire

The axis is DEAD, declared, if **either** of these fires:

1. **Arithmetic:** the measured cost per round, read from the run's own
   `cost-matched null: ... cost X% of R` line, is `>= 5.0% of R` on both
   markets. The gate demands `expectancy >= +0.050R = 5.0% of R`, so a cost at
   or above that level means one round's spread eats the entire edge the gate
   asks for, and no 15m mechanism on this instrument at this spread can clear
   it whatever the mechanism is. If this fires, the conclusion stands without
   reading a single PF.
2. **Gate:** 0 of the declared primary cells clear `PF >= 1.200` AND
   `expectancy >= +0.050R` with `>= 40` trades on **both** windows.

The axis is ALIVE only if at least one construct clears both gate legs with
>= 40 trades on window A **and** window B, on the same market.

A result of "this axis opens nothing" is the registered, acceptable outcome.
No cell will be promoted for looking good on one window.

**Declared total: 104 cells (run x row).** The multiple-testing ledger at the

## How it will be read

- Gate (not adjustable): `PF >= 1.200` AND `expectancy >= +0.050R`.
- Minimum sample: 40 trades. Below that, nothing is concluded — the record's
  own CRT case gave `PF 1.753 / pct 96 / 14 trades` and
  `PF 0.682 / pct 8 / 178 trades` from the same rule on the same window, and
  the only difference was sample size.
- Both windows required: A `2025-07-01 → 2025-10-01`, B `2025-04-01 →
  2025-07-01`. One window is nothing.
- Every percentile written will carry its `null p50` next to it. The desk's
  null median PF is 0.867, below 1, so a high percentile means "loses less
  than random entry at the same cost, count and side ratio", not "makes money".
- `--exit-mix` is on, so the share of positions closed by the 4-hour
  `max_hold_ms` cap is read before any row is called dead on its merits.

## Known confound, stated before the run

`gold-intraday` carries `weekdays` (Mo–Fr on the New York clock) and
`flat:1630-1815`. On a market that never closes, those two gates discard
roughly two days in seven plus 1h45 a day for a reason that belongs to gold's
session, not to BTC. The primary arm is therefore "the gold mechanism as
written, moved to BTC" and NOT "BTC measured 24/7"; the secondary arm is the
second thing. Both will be labelled as such.

## What each role said

- **data-integrity:** NO OBJECTION — both parquet files checked for existence
  and coverage before registration; `news:` source will be quoted from the run
  header, not assumed.
- **adversary:** the cost arithmetic must be read from the tool's own printed
  line, not computed by hand from `default.toml`, or it is a retyped number.
- **risk:** no live consequence; measurement only.

## What would reopen this

A measured BTC spread materially below the configured one (a venue, account or
tier where `spread / stop` falls under ~2% of R), or a mechanism whose stop is
wide enough that the same absolute spread is a small fraction of R — the
break-even spread will be stated so this is checkable rather than rhetorical.

## What this does not say

Nothing here is a claim about BTC on timeframes other than 15m, about holds
longer than the engine's 4-hour cap, or about any instrument other than the
two named files.

## Addendum, 2026-10-06 — the result

**Outcome:** the axis is NOT refuted by cost arithmetic — the premise it was
handed was wrong by a factor of four on the one BTC tape with no gap — and it
produced exactly one both-window gate pass out of 52 construct-market pairs,
which then died at the first repricing. The mechanism question is closed; the
cost question is closed in the opposite direction from the one expected.

### 1. Cost per round, in R — measured, both markets

`cost_table.exe --data=/e/rust/flowdesk/data --to=2025-10-01`
(`receipts/2026-10-06-btc-247/cost-table.txt`), rule I = the registration's own
horizon (`stop = 1.5 x ATR14` on 15m bars), spread/stop with lots and contract
size cancelling:

| market | file | charged spread | median stop | **cost, % of R** | p10 – p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `btc` | BTCUSDT-15m | 5.00 USD (ASSUMED, never measured) | 376.2 pts | **1.329%** | 0.702 – 2.525% |
| `btcusd` | BTCUSD-15m | 17.05 USD (one terminal read) | 332.2 pts | **5.133%** | 2.647 – 11.540% |
| `xauusd` | XAUUSD-15m | 0.28 | 3.747 pts | **7.472%** | 3.790 – 13.213% |

Common era every instrument covers (2024-09-12 to 2025-09-30), the only row on
which instruments are comparable: `btc` **1.329%**, `btcusd` **4.410%**,
`xauusd` **5.010%**, `xauduka` **4.912%**.

The per-row lines inside the hypotheses receipts agree: `btc` ran
**0.75% – 1.99% of R** across the 13 constructs in window A and
**0.74% – 1.77%** in window B; `btcusd` ran **2.50% – 6.22%** in A and
**2.33% – 5.96%** in B.

**The figures the brief carried — "BTC 5.16% weekday, 9.96% weekend, 5-9x
gold's 1.06%" — are not reproduced and are not traceable in this record.**
`9.96% of R` appears in four receipts under `docs/`, and every one of them is
**xauusd** at a 2.81-point stop (`2026-10-02-drift-null/screen-in-sample-*`,
`2026-10-04-crt/B-out-of-sample-cells`). No BTC receipt in `docs/` carries
either number, and nothing in the tree carries `1.06% of R` at all. The 5.16%
is close to `btcusd`'s pooled rule-I figure of 5.133%, so the most likely
reading is that a BTCUSD **CFD** figure was carried across to BTC as a whole
and compared against a **gold multi-day** stop (xauusd rule M = 0.690%, rule H
= 1.808%). At a matched stop rule and a matched era, BTC is the **cheaper**
instrument: the Binance tape by 3.8x and even the CFD slightly, against gold's
5.010%.

So the arithmetic falsifier (leg 1: cost >= 5.0% of R on both markets) **did
not fire**. `btc` is at 1.329%, a quarter of the +0.050R the gate asks for.
Cost per R is not a property of the instrument; it is `spread / stop`, and the
stop moves with the horizon, not with the ticker.

### 3. The one survivor, taken apart

Four things, each from its own printed line, each of which would be enough:

- **It is a cost knife-edge, not a mechanism.** Repriced with `--spread=` and
  nothing else changed, window B's profit factor goes 1.228 (5.00 USD) to
  **1.186 (8.00)**, 1.183 (10), 1.177 (12), 1.155 (17.05), 1.171 (30), 1.090
  (50). It fails the 1.200 leg **at 8.00 USD of spread**, 3 USD above the
  Binance assumption, which is 0.44% of R. The CFD's own 17.05 kills it.
- **It only exists on an instrument the desk cannot trade.** The cost table
  says of the 5.00: "ASSUMED, never measured (and not an account this desk
  trades)". The same construct on `btcusd`, the CFD the desk does have, fails
  both windows (A PF 1.184, B PF 1.148).
- **It is nothing against its own matched null, and on one window the
  percentile is void.** Window A: count match 0.71 and cost match 0.63, both
  printed as `** outside the band: this percentile is unmatched **` — so the
  92% is not a reading. Window B: percentile 60% against a null whose median PF
  is 1.162 and whose p95 is 1.746, i.e. the method sits just above the middle
  of its own control.
- **The 4-hour cap is doing part of the work.** `--exit-mix` puts **29% of its
  window-A exits on TIMEOUT** (34 of 117), the highest share of any row
  measured here. Across all eight batch runs the cap closed 6.8% to 11.1% of
  positions.

### 4. What the gold filters were discarding, measured

Counted directly off the parquet on the New York clock (both windows lie inside
2025 EDT, so NY = UTC minus 4 exactly):

| market | window | bars | vs 24/7 calendar | `weekdays` drops Sat+Sun | `flat:1630-1815` drops | both filters | `--guards` weekend flat drops |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `btc` | A | 8,832 | **100.0%** | 2,496 (28.3%) | 644 (7.3%) | **33.5%** | 1,625 (18.4%) |
| `btc` | B | 8,736 | **100.0%** | 2,496 (28.6%) | 637 (7.3%) | **33.8%** | 1,625 (18.6%) |
| `btcusd` | A | 8,672 | 98.2% | 2,408 (27.8%) | 627 (7.2%) | 32.9% | 1,465 (16.9%) |
| `btcusd` | B | 8,535 | 97.7% | 2,389 (28.0%) | 617 (7.2%) | 33.1% | 1,424 (16.7%) |

So the primary arm measured a third of the tape thrown away for a reason that
belongs to gold's session. Removing both filters raised trade counts by about a
quarter (A `btc`: 1,290 to 1,629 trades across the 13 rows) and changed which
cells passed, but it did not produce a surviving mechanism.

**And the premise "BTC has no weekend gap" is true of exactly one of the two
files.** BTCUSDT-15m has **zero gaps longer than 15 minutes** in either window
(8,832 of 8,832 and 8,736 of 8,736 slots present). BTCUSD-15m, the CFD, is
missing **40.0 hours in window A and 50.2 hours in window B**, and every one of
its largest gaps starts at a Friday 20:45 UTC bar — Friday 16:45 New York,
within five minutes of where gold's own `WEEKEND_FLAT` guard sits. The one BTC
instrument this desk could actually trade has a weekly break too. Whether those
gaps are a venue close or a collection outage is not established here; what is
measured is that they exist and when they start.

Note also that `--guards` keeps `flat_before_weekend_hhmm = 1640`, which blocks
Friday from 16:40 NY and **all of Saturday** but not Sunday
(`past_weekend_cutoff`: `weekday == 5 && minute >= cutoff || weekday == 6`), so
even the secondary arm never saw a Saturday. A genuinely 24/7 run needs
`--guards` off, which was not measured.

### 5. Falsifier

- **Leg 1 (arithmetic) did NOT fire** and could not: `btc` costs 1.329% of R at
  this horizon, not >= 5.0%. The brief's premise that one round's spread
  already exceeds the gate's whole edge is true of `btcusd` (5.133%) and false
  of `btc` by a factor of four.
- **Leg 2 (gate) fired on `btcusd` and did not fire on `btc`.** On `btcusd`,
  0 of 26 construct pairs cleared both windows and 2 of 52 cells cleared
  one — the axis is dead on the only tradable BTC instrument here. On `btc`,
  one pair cleared both windows, so leg 2 did not fire by the letter of the
  registration; it then failed the first cost repricing at 8.00 USD, which is
  the stated reason it is not carried forward.

### 6. Break-even spread, as promised

On the only construct that got far enough to have one: at window B's measured
stop of 682.8 points, the `PF >= 1.200` leg breaks between **5.00 and 8.00 USD**
of round-trip spread, that is between **0.73% and 1.17% of R**. Generally, for
a 15m BTC method whose stop is ~330–380 points, holding the whole +0.050R edge
in cost requires spread <= **0.05 x stop = 16.6–18.8 USD**, which `btcusd`'s
17.05 sits exactly on and `btc`'s 5.00 clears with room. The binding constraint
on BTC at 15m is therefore **not** the spread. It is that no mechanism here
produced an edge that survived both windows at any spread.
