## Is CRT distinguishable from what the record has already closed? The code, read

### From `pdhl` mode 0: **yes, on six specific lines, and they matter**

`crates/fd-strategy/src/pdhl.rs` mode 0 is the nearest thing in the registry
and its own doc comment is nearly the CRT sentence: "a bar trades through the
level and *closes back inside* the day's range — the touch was absorbed." Read
against `crates/fd-strategy/src/crt.rs`:

| | `pdhl` mode 0 | `crt` |
|---|---|---|
| what defines the range | the previous **New York** day, `previous_day_range` scanning back over `ny_day(b.time)` | a completed bucket of a **chosen** timeframe, `bar.time.div_euclid(htf_ms)`, 1H / 4H / 1D, **UTC**-aligned |
| what sweeps it | **any single entry bar** at **any time during the whole day**: `if b.high > pdh && b.close < pdh` on the 15-minute bar | the **immediately following candle of the same timeframe**, aggregated: `c2.high > c1.high && c2.close < c1.high` |
| where the stop goes | beyond that **15-minute bar's** extreme plus a buffer | beyond that **higher-timeframe candle's** extreme plus the same buffer |
| the target | `bar.close ± risk × riskReward`, a **constant multiple** | candle 1's **opposite extreme**, or its midpoint, a **variable** distance |
| how many signals | one per level per day, so **two a day** are possible and the two can be on opposite sides | one per range candle, and a candle that sweeps both extremes is **discarded** (`if took_high == took_low { return Intent::None }`) |
| when it may fire | the moment a 15-minute bar closes back inside | only on the **last entry bar of candle 2's bucket** |

Those are not one mechanism with different parameters. The measured
consequence, from diagnostic D3 — `pdhl` mode 0 at its own defaults on CRT's
own two windows, same spread, same guards, same null: **965 trades on Window A
and 1,051 on Window B** against CRT's 1D cells' **220 and 211** once their
risk ceiling is lifted. CRT at daily granularity takes **a fifth of `pdhl`'s
trades**, because requiring the sweep to happen in the single day immediately
after the range day throws away four fifths of the touches `pdhl` takes. They
are different methods and the counts prove it.

`pdhl` mode 0 also fails on both windows — PF 0.812 / expectancy −0.191 R at
the 61st percentile on Window A, PF 0.885 / −0.129 R at the 59th on Window B —
which reproduces the three-month programme's refutation on new windows. But its
percentile is **higher** than CRT's 1H cells' (0th to 26th), so CRT is not a
better version of it on any axis measured here.

### From `ict-sweep-mss-fvg`: **neither a subset nor a superset. They intersect and neither contains the other**

`crates/fd-strategy/src/ict.rs`, read in full. Its sweep test looks superficially
identical —

```rust
let (extreme, depth, closed_inside) = match side {
    Side::Short => (bar.high, bar.high - level, bar.close < level),
    ...
if !(closed_inside && depth > sweep_buffer && depth >= min_sweep && depth <= max_sweep) {
```

— and is a different predicate in four ways, each of which breaks containment
in one direction:

1. **`level` is a confirmed swing pivot, not a candle boundary.** It comes from
   `ctx.s_back(SWING_HIGH, …)`, the `swing(3,3)` fractal, and must be no older
   than `liquidityLookback = 150` bars. A CRT range candle's extreme need not be
   a swing pivot, and a swing pivot need not be any candle's bucket extreme.
   **So neither spec implies the other even at step one.**
2. **The sweep is a single entry bar** (`bars[s]`), not an aggregated candle. A
   higher-timeframe candle can sweep and close back inside with no single
   15-minute bar inside it doing both.
3. **The depth is bounded above**: `maxSweepPips = 60` at `pipSize = 0.1`, so
   6.00 price units. CRT has no upper bound, and its 1D cells' realised median
   stop is 11.04 to 17.22 price units — **two to three times ict's entire
   admissible sweep depth.** Those trades are outside `ict`'s specification
   altogether.
4. **Four further conditions and five deadlines.** An unfilled higher-timeframe
   fair value gap with the sweep extreme inside it (`nearest_htf_gap` + the
   `htfTolerancePips` test); a protected swing strictly on the far side; a
   displacement close through it within `maxBarsSweepToMss = 20` bars with
   `body >= avg_body * 1.5` **and** `body >= 0.5 × ATR`; a lower-timeframe gap
   in that impulse within 5 bars; a first touch of its 50% within 30 bars. CRT
   requires none of these, and entry is `maxBarsSweepToMss + maxBarsMssToFvg +
   maxBarsFvgToEntry` up to **55 bars after the sweep** instead of at candle 2's
   close.

So `ict-sweep-mss-fvg` is much stricter *on the sweeps it shares with CRT* —
which is why `xau-ict-5m` has taken 0 trades — while CRT admits a whole class of
deep higher-timeframe sweeps that `ict` excludes by `maxSweepPips`. **The
intersection is the phrase "traded through a level and closed back inside" and
nothing else in either file.** This was read, not assumed.

## What a percentile is here, and it is not profitability

**On all nine cells and both windows, the null's own median loses money.** The
receipts print it on every row: `crt/1h-opposite`'s Window B null has a median
profit factor of **0.882** and its 95th is **0.949** — still a loss. So "the
77th percentile" on `crt/4h-opposite` means **"loses less than 77% of random
entries matched on count, cost, side ratio and time in market"**, not "makes
money". Nine cells sat at or above the 95th while losing money in the record
this desk is correcting; this registration therefore printed the control's own
profit factor in every table above, which is why the two columns `null p50 PF`
and `null p95 PF` are there.

The gate is what says whether a thing makes money, and it is the gate that
every cell failed.

## Attacking my own result: three diagnostics, declared before they were run

Three programmes on this desk have closed with zero survivors, so the base rate
says a survivor would more likely be a defect in my measurement than an edge.
Nothing survived — so the attacks went the other way, at whether the
**refutation** is sound. Two of the three found real defects, and they are in
my own design.

### D2: the risk ceiling broke my own `rangeTf` axis, and it is the most useful thing in this study

The registration fixed `maxRiskAtr = 3.0` by fiat, copied from `pdhl`. **The
ATR is measured on fifteen-minute bars while a 1D cell's stop spans a daily
sweep.** One absolute risk ceiling is therefore not the same filter at 1H as at
1D, which means the registration's timeframe axis was never purely a comparison
of timeframes. The receipts said so before I ran anything else: the 1D cells'
realised median stop was **2.738 ATR against a ceiling of 3.000**, pressed flat
against the limit.

Lifting the ceiling to a value that cannot bind
(`2026-10-04-crt-ceiling-lifted.toml`, nine rows, declared as unable to produce
a survivor):

| cell | trades, declared | trades, ceiling lifted | PF, lifted | expectancy, lifted | pct, lifted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `1d-opposite` A | 15 | **220** | 0.724 | −0.053 R | 20% |
| `1d-mid` A | 15 | **168** | 0.662 | −0.067 R | 10% |
| `1d-rr15` A | 15 | **220** | 0.792 | −0.040 R | 36% |
| `1d-opposite` B | 14 | **211** | 0.762 | −0.045 R | 12% |
| `1d-mid` B | 14 | **178** | **0.682** | **−0.071 R** | **8%** |
| `1d-rr15` B | 14 | **211** | 0.759 | −0.046 R | 11% |
| 1H and 4H cells | — | −0.4% to +28.9% | within 0.054 PF of the declared cells | — | — |

**The ceiling was refusing about 93% of the 1D setups.** The declared 1D cells
are not thin results, they are an artefact, and the registration's timeframe
axis is confounded to that extent. This is reported, not repaired: lifting a
declared parameter after seeing a result is the move this programme forbids, so
none of these rows is a survivor and all nine are counted as looks.

**And the single most useful number in the study:** `crt/1d-mid` on Window B
reads profit factor **1.753**, expectancy **+0.127 R**, **96th percentile** on
14 trades — and **0.682, −0.071 R, 8th percentile** on 178 trades of exactly
the same rule in exactly the same window. Nothing changed but the risk ceiling
that was throwing 93% of the sample away. If this registration had declared
`maxRiskAtr` differently, or had quoted the 14-trade row, it would have
reported a 96th-percentile result on a rule that loses 7.1% of R per trade.
That is how a 20.6%-at-the-95th record gets built, demonstrated on one row in
one window.

### A third defect, found by reading the clock rather than a receipt

A 1D cell's signal is by construction the **23:45 UTC** bar and its fill is by
construction the **00:00 UTC** open, the thinnest hour of the gold session. The
0.28 spread charged to it was measured over 12.8 hours of mostly liquid time,
so the 1D cells' costs are **understated by an unknown amount**. This affects
only the cells already reported as unmeasured, and it points the same way: the
1D figures are, if anything, better than the truth.

### What the attacks did NOT find

- **The implementation's setup frequency is scale-invariant**, which is the
  check that says the bucketing is right rather than off by a bucket. With the
  ceiling lifted, the mechanism fires on 21.0% / 22.9% / 17.7% of consecutive
  candle pairs at 1H / 4H / 1D on Window A and 21.2% / 21.7% / 15.9% on
  Window B. A bucketing error, an off-by-one in "the last bar of the bucket",
  or a look-ahead would not produce one rate across three timeframes, two
  venues and eight and a quarter years of calendar.
- **No look-ahead.** `the_signal_on_bar_i_does_not_change_when_later_bars_change`
  rewrites every bar after the signal and asserts the intent is identical.
  "Last bar of the bucket" is decided from that bar's own timestamp and the
  series' own step, never from a later bar.
- **The null is matched where it matters.** On the six cells with a usable
  trade count, the count match is 0.78–1.06, the cost match 0.86–1.20 and the
  achieved signed share of time within 0.017 of the method's. Four of the
  twelve are outside the time-in-market band (1.79 to 2.65, all on the
  `mid`-target cells, whose holds are the shortest) and are printed unmatched;
  none of them could have passed anyway, having failed the gate.
- **Two of my own unit tests were wrong when first written** and both failures
  were mine. The 1.5R target is *nearer* than the opposite extreme on the test
  fixture (22.95 against 30.00 price units), and a sixteen-bar fixture left
  ATR(14) at NaN, which silently disabled the very risk ceiling the test was
  asserting. Both are recorded in `fb4ded8`. The second one is the same class
  of defect as D2 and I found it in a test before I found it in a result.
