## The short answer

**There is no single answer, and the four claims take three different letters.
That is the finding.** The question "why did they die on the second window"
assumes one cause; the measurements say the four claims died of three unrelated
things, and one of them did not die at all.

| claim | letter | the figure that decides it |
|---|---|---|
| `fri` gold weekend hold | **(a)**, by a mis-specified null rather than by search | its first window's whole effect is the unconditional drift: raw **+1.5327 $/oz per hold** (t +5.00), **−0.0128 $/oz per hold (t −0.04)** once the window's own drift x the hold's 50.0 hours is removed. The drift-controlled excess is **larger on the second window** (+0.4378 $/oz, t +2.21). |
| `eu` euro European hours | **(a) or (b), not separated** | (d) is out: power **0.997**. (c) is out by **0.01 pips per hold**. (a) stands or falls on which family the row belongs to: p = 0.00147 survives its declared family of 3 (0.0044) and fails the programme's 247 (0.363). |
| `xag20` silver 20-day sign | **(a)**, on the record's own pre-registered arithmetic | its own decision already published **Bonferroni 3 x 0.024 = 0.072 > 0.05** — it never cleared the multiplicity its own registration declared. (d) is **unresolved** by this registration's rule: power 0.734, inside the (0.50, 0.80) band. |
| `close` gold across the New York close | **(c) and (d) jointly — it never died** | it keeps a drift-controlled excess of **+1.1273 / +1.2487 / +1.7457 basis points per hold** on three windows spanning 16 years (chi-square 0.253 on 2 d.f.), while the 0.28 price-unit cost falls from **2.09 bp to 0.70 bp**. Its second window's power to detect its own first-window estimate is **0.486** — below this registration's 0.50 floor. |

**What could not be separated:** (a) from (b) on `eu`, and (d) from everything
else on `xag20`. The measurements that would separate them are named in §7.

**The one thing all four have in common** is not a market change. It is that the
instrument used to call them real — "at or above the 95th percentile of its own
null" — passed **51 of 247 cells (20.6%)** in the programme that produced them,
against the 5% a calibrated null gives. Nine of those 51 cells **lost money**
while sitting at the 95th or above. The percentile was never an edge test.

nulls were not run with enough draws to produce a small enough number.** That is
not a statement about gold; it is a statement about how many seeds a null needs,
and it costs nothing to fix.

## 3. (d) — the power of each second window, in profit-factor units

σ = (null p95 − null p50) / 1.645 from the second window's **own published
receipt**; power = Φ((θ₁ − p50₂)/σ₂ − 1.645), one-sided α = 0.05, θ₁ the first
window's own point estimate.

| claim | n (window 2) | σ of its null | power to detect window 1's estimate | smallest profit factor window 2 could detect at 0.80 power | letter on (d) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `fri` | 400 holds | 0.1356 | **1.000** | 1.136 (null median 0.799) | ruled out |
| `eu` sized null | 2,057 holds | 0.0541 | **0.942** | 1.030 (0.895) | ruled out |
| `eu` direction null | 2,057 holds | 0.0614 | **0.859** | 1.055 (0.902) | ruled out |
| `xag20` | 186 trades | 0.1726 | **0.734** | 1.378 (0.949) | **unresolved** (0.50–0.80 band) |
| `close` | 282 holds | 0.2249 | **0.486** | 1.451 (0.892) | **(d) SUPPORTED** |

**In the units `eu`'s own registration declared.** The published per-hold standard
deviations reproduce the published t-statistics exactly, which is the check that
this arithmetic is the registration's own:

```
window 1  2010-06 -> 2018-06   n=2088  SD 40.8 pips  SE 0.8929  observed -2.84  t = -3.18  (published: -3.18)
window 2  2018-06 -> 2026-05   n=2056  SD 27.5 pips  SE 0.6065  observed -0.10  t = -0.16  (published: -0.16)
```

Window 2's power, two-sided α = 0.05:

- to detect a true **−2.84 pips/hold** (window 1's own estimate): **0.997**
- to detect a true **−1.50 pips/hold** (the registration's own gate): **0.696**
- to detect a true **−1.00 pips/hold**: **0.378**
- smallest excess it could have detected at 0.80 power: **−1.699 pips/hold**

**So the euro's second window could not have missed window 1's effect — 3 chances
in 1,000. (d) is dead for `eu`, by two independent routes that agree.** The
adversary on that record had already said so in words (*"a true −1.5 would have
printed t ≈ −2.5"*); this is the number.

**The one place (d) is alive is the claim that is still standing.** `close`'s
second window — 282 holds of Vantage bars — had power **0.486** and could not have
detected a profit factor below **1.451**. It printed 1.299 and the record read that
as "keeps its sign and place on the second window". It is not a confirmation. It
is a window that could not answer.

Gold's unconditional drift, restated per window, in ATR20 per 5 sessions:

| `xauduka` 2010-06 → 2018-06 (`fri`'s **second** window) | 2,479 | **+0.0224 ATR20 / 5 sessions** | **+0.69** |
| `xauduka` 2018-06 → 2025-04 (`fri`'s **first** window) | 2,096 | **+0.2198 ATR20 / 5 sessions** | **+6.20** |

**The drift is ten times larger on the first window than on the second, and
nothing in either null controls for it.** That alone predicts the shape the whole
programme kept finding.

### `fri`, the gold weekend hold: the first window **is** the drift

100% long by construction — 337 / 403 / 68 entries, no short entries on any
window. Flipping the sides negates every figure, so the flipped test is the table
below with its signs reversed.

| window | holds | mean hold | raw move | minus the window's own drift | minus the hold's own week |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1: 2018-06 → 2025-04 | 337 | 50.0 h | **+1.5327 $/oz (t +5.00)** | **−0.0128 $/oz (t −0.04)** | +0.2369 (t +0.33) |
| 2: 2010-06 → 2018-06 | 403 | 50.1 h | +0.4959 $/oz (t +2.50) | **+0.4378 $/oz (t +2.21)** | +0.2998 (t +0.63) |
| 3: 2025-04 → 2026-09 | 68 | 49.8 h | +4.0265 $/oz (t +1.10) | −0.4842 $/oz (t −0.13) | −1.1486 (t −0.22) |

The arithmetic that closes it: window 1's unconditional drift is
**+0.03089 $/oz per hour**; over the hold's 50.0 hours that is **+1.5445 $/oz**.
The hold earned **+1.5327 $/oz**, which is **99.2%** of what simply being long for
50 hours earned in that window, and not one basis point more.

**So `fri` did not decay, was not mis-tested, and was not under-powered. Its
first-window profit factor of 2.064 at the 100th percentile of 1,000 permuted
sides is the 2018–2025 gold trend, held across a weekend, compared against a
control that flips a coin for its side.** This registration's drift clause
therefore overrides the percentile, as it said it would: the claim is the drift and
is reported as the drift.

The inversion is worth stating because it is the opposite of what the record
believes. **The drift-controlled excess is positive on the window the record
closed and absent on the window the record passed:** +0.4378 $/oz per hold
(t +2.21) on 2010–2018 against −0.0128 (t −0.04) on 2018–2025. I attacked that
figure rather than reporting it as a finding, and it dies on cost: the spread is
0.28 $/oz, leaving **+0.1578 $/oz per hold net, t = 0.80** — $63.60 an ounce over
eight years, unstopped through 403 closed markets. It is not a method and nothing
here proposes it as one.

### `close` and `xag20` are not the drift

`close` keeps its sign and nearly all its size under detrending — §6's table in the
stricter test shows +1.5453 bp raw against +1.2487 bp detrended on 2018–2025, so
19% of the raw was the drift and 81% was not — and on 2010–2018, where there is no
drift to remove, raw +1.1422 bp and detrended +1.1273 bp are the same number.
`xag20` held balanced sides in a window with no drift (§4). Only `fri` is the
drift, and it is the one the record was proudest of.

### For completeness: gold's own trend sign has the same defect

Measured the same way, the gold 20-day sign on 2018-06 → 2026-05 held **59.1%
long** in a window whose unconditional step was **+1.62 $/oz per weekday
(t +2.27)**. Its return falls from **+0.5667 $/oz per step (t +0.79)** to
**+0.2723 (t +0.38)** once the drift the held side rode is removed; long steps
+1.8507 against short steps −1.2876. The `tsmom` family on gold inherits defect 1
whole.

## 7. What I could not separate, and the measurement that would

**`eu`: (a) against (b).** Its second window had 0.997 power and saw nothing, so
the effect is not present there at window 1's size. Whether window 1's
t = −3.18 was a real 2011–2016 regime that ended (b) or a 3-standard-error draw on
an early pre-registered row (a) cannot be decided from this pair, because the two
hypotheses predict the same two windows. The record itself says the sign flipped in
2025 and that **no single year is individually significant**, so a date-split
cannot locate a change, and (b) therefore fails this registration's own test for
(b) — which leaves (a) and (b) both live and neither established.

**The one measurement that separates them:** the same claim on a **different pair,
registered independently, on both windows at once** — sterling through London hours
or the yen through Tokyo hours, one row, the committed detrending script, the
effect size pre-declared at −1.5 pips per hold, and the **same two windows**. If
the local-hours sign is a property of currencies it will show on window 1 and fade
on window 2 on a second pair; if `eu` was a draw, the second pair shows nothing on
either window. That is a question about the world and costs one download. It is
**not** `data-sealed/`, and it is not proposed as a strategy under any outcome.

**`xag20`: (d) is unresolved at power 0.734.** It does not matter, because (a) is
established for it on its own published arithmetic. Resolving it anyway would need
roughly **2.2x the trades** — about 410 — which on a 13.8-day hold is 15 years of
silver, and no silver window on disk is unspent.

**`close`: (c) and (d) are both live and they are not rivals.** The cost fell
(c) **and** the newest window is too small to resolve the row (d). §8 measures both
in one test.

row is not the drift**, which is what separates it from `fri`.

**F1 — UNDECIDED, by the rule I wrote down.** Inverse-variance weighted means and
a chi-square across the three windows on 2 degrees of freedom:

```
detrended excess in dollars an ounce   weighted mean +0.1811 $/oz   chi-square 2.673
detrended excess in basis points       weighted mean +1.2000 bp     chi-square 0.253
```

5.99 is the 5% point. **Neither unit is rejected**, so F1 does not discriminate and
I do not claim it did. The reason is the third window: 292 holds give a standard
error of 0.6296 $/oz, wide enough to be consistent with anything. What the numbers
do show, without the test's authority, is the consistency of the basis-point
reading: **+1.1273, +1.2487 and +1.7457 bp, chi-square 0.253 — the close-to-reopen
drift in gold is one number, about 1.2 basis points of price per 1.7-hour hold,
across sixteen years and two different feeds.** That is the most reproducible thing
in this record, and it is still not a method.

**F2 — FIRED, on all three windows. This is the verdict.** The row is a method on
a window only if its basis-point excess net of the basis-point cost is positive
with t >= 2.0:

- 2010–2018: **−0.9661 bp, t = −3.50** — FAIL, and significantly negative
- 2018–2025: **−0.3442 bp, t = −1.28** — FAIL
- 2025–2026: **+1.0490 bp, t = +0.75** — FAIL, positive and indistinguishable from zero

**The effect has never paid its cost on any window, including the newest.** What
changed between the windows is the cost, from 2.09 bp to 0.70 bp, while the effect
stayed at about 1.2 bp. At today's price the effect is 1.75x the cost for the first
time in sixteen years — and the window where that is true has **292 holds**, which
is why the t is 0.75.

**The power of the window that looks like a pass.** The per-hold standard deviation
of the net basis-point figure on the newest window is **24.02 bp**. At the observed
mean of +1.0490 bp, reaching t = 2.0 needs **2,097 holds — about 10 years of
Monday-to-Thursday sessions.** It has 292. The smallest net excess those 292 holds
could resolve at t = 2.0 is **+2.811 bp**, which is 2.3x the effect's own size. So
the honest statement about the only claim still standing is: **nobody on this desk
can say whether it pays at the current price level, the existing window is a
quarter of the way to being able to say, and the thing that would answer it is more
sessions at this price, not another method.**

**Both measures, as required.** `net_usd` and the basis-point figure give the same
ordering on all three windows here and the same three FAILs, so nothing in this
conclusion depends on which is quoted. The conclusion rests on the **basis-point**
figure, because the question was whether a constant absolute cost is a constant
test, and only a proportional measure can answer that.

## 9. What this does not say

- It does not say the gold close-to-reopen drift is not real. It is the one
  drift-controlled effect in this record that replicates on three windows and two
  feeds, at about 1.2 basis points of price per hold. It says the drift has never
  been worth the spread on any window measured, and that the window where it might
  be cannot tell.
- It does not say the 2026-09-13/14 programme was badly run. Its screen declared
  its own 170 looks and called its own result noise; its adversary pre-empted
  several of the figures above in words. What it lacked was a null that controls
  for drift and enough null draws to produce a p-value that survives its own
  count.
- It does not say `eu` was noise. Its first window was 3 standard errors from zero
  on a drift-controlled statistic with a committed script. It says that whether
  that was a regime or a draw is not decidable from the two windows on disk.
- **It does not propose anything for the funded account, paper or live.** No
  threshold in `config/default.toml` or `ui/src/lib/verdicts.ts` was touched; no
  build was run; `data-sealed/` was not opened, and nothing here is worth spending
  it on — the measurement §7 names is a different pair, not a held-back window.
