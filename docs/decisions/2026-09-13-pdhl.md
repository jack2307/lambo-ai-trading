# Previous-day high/low: both the fade and the break lose more than random

**Date:** 2026-09-13
**Question:** Are yesterday's New York high and low levels that either absorb
a touch (fade, Asian hours) or give way to a close through (break, New York
morning)? (`docs/hypotheses/2026-09-13-pdhl.md`, pre-registered at commit
`5ba8832`'s parent; first run on the long window as primary.)
**Outcome:** No, on the primary window, decisively: every row fails the gate
and sits at the 1st–6th percentile of a null gated to its own session —
worse than random entry in the same hours. The confirmation window was not
opened; there was nothing to confirm. Closed.

## What was measured

- Method: `pdhl` (new), fade and break modes, stop beyond the touch bar's
  extreme (fade) or at the level (break) plus `bufferPips`, target a risk
  multiple, first signal per level per day. Four unit tests including
  no-future.
- Primary window `xauduka:5m` 2022-06-16 → 2025-04-10 (198,716 bars), the
  first pass under the "long window first" rule. Nulls gated to
  `sessions:1900-0200` / all day / `hours:0800-1200`. Direction nulls per
  preset with the same filters (the `--filters` fix from the London pass).
- Receipts: `docs/research/runs/2026-09-13-pdhl/`.

Direction nulls: fade-asia 0.593 → 61st; fade-allday 0.664 → 47th;
break-nyam 0.643 → 82nd. Inside, all three.

## Reading

A fade and a break of the same level cannot both be bad *directional*
calls — the direction nulls agree, all inside. What both share is the stop:
a few pips beyond the bar that touched the level. Against a $0.28 spread a
stop of a dollar or two makes the round trip a third of the risk before the
market moves, and a level that is where everyone's stops sit is also where
this model's stop sits. The losses are cost and stop placement, not
information about the level; but a level whose only tradeable reading needs
a stop wide enough to survive the sweep is not a level with an edge at this
cost. (Median stop distance was not measured; the arithmetic is the reading,
not a receipt.)

## What each role said

Reviews were not spawned: nothing survived any stage, no row is above the
10th percentile of its null, and the mechanism is arithmetic. The adversary's
standing question for a negative — is it an artefact? — is answered by the
unit tests (stops and targets land where the spec says) and by both modes
losing the same way. If that is wrong, the record is the place to say so.

## What would reopen this

A pre-registered variant with the stop at a structural distance (an ATR
multiple, or the level's opposite side) rather than the touch bar's wick —
a different hypothesis about where the stop belongs, with the same nulls.
Not a different session.

## What this does not say

- It does not say the levels are unwatched; it says touching them with a
  wick-tight stop loses to the spread.
- It does not say the 2025–26 window would agree; it was not opened and,
  after the London pass, would not have been believed alone.
