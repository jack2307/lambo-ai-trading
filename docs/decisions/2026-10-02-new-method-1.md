# 2026-10-02-new-method-1: three mechanisms that do not read gold's own price path, and the one that cleared everything in sample and inverted out of it

**Registration:** `docs/hypotheses/2026-10-02-new-method-1.md`, commit `2e76707`, written
and committed **before any measurement**, with a dated amendment appended afterwards that
records a deviation rather than hiding it.
**Status:** decided. **All three mechanisms are abandoned, each by a falsifier named in
advance.** 24 cells declared, 24 run, 0 survivors. Nothing is proposed, nothing goes near
a funded account, and **the sealed hold-out was not read.**

## What was designed, and why not another textbook mechanism

The owner's standing instruction is to design methods rather than sweep the registry's 21
textbook mechanisms again. `docs/decisions/2026-09-23-three-month-search.md` swept 161
cells over those 21 and carried 25 out of sample with 0 survivors;
`docs/decisions/2026-09-24-designed-methods.md` asked four agents to design their own and
closed with 0 submissions.

The design constraint I set myself came from the sharpest measurement in that record, which
is not a backtest. Angle 3's Lo–MacKinlay variance ratios on 362,802 fifteen-minute returns
and 3,957 session returns sit **within 1–7% of 1.0 at every horizon from 30 minutes to 60
sessions**. Read plainly: gold's own past returns carry no linear information about its
future returns at any horizon this desk trades. Every one of the 21 registry mechanisms, and
three of the four designed ones, reads gold's own price path and nothing else.

So: **the trigger must be something other than gold's own price path.** The three
mechanisms are triggered by a published release calendar, a published release calendar, and
the exchange's opening hours. A variance ratio of 1.0 is silent about all three, because a
calendar effect on 2.7% of sessions and a liquidity-hour effect both average out of an
unconditional autocorrelation.

Two of the three hold for sessions rather than hours, because angle 3 measured the cost bar
falling 21× from an intraday stop (14.0% of R) to a swing stop (0.67% of R).

## The cost gate, derived in advance rather than asserted

`[markets.xauusd] spread = 0.28` price units per ounce, sampled 2,315 times, half on entry
and half on exit: **0.28 price units round trip.** For a stop of one daily ATR20, R is one
daily ATR20; the design window's ATR20d averages **17.62 price units**, so cost ≈ 0.0159 R
and the standing 0.05 R expectancy needs gross **0.066 ATR20d ≈ 1.2 price units per trade**.

Three legs, fixed before any run, evaluated on the **net** mean:
1. net mean ≥ 0.066 ATR20d; 2. **t ≥ 3.0**; 3. sign holds in ≥ 3 of 4 disjoint sub-eras.

The registration named which leg it expected to bind — **legs 2 and 3, not leg 1** — and
said so because 1.2 price units is 6.6% of a daily range and a three-session hold clears it
on noise about half the time. That prediction held: every failure below is a leg 2 or leg 3
failure, and leg 1 passed in three of the four cells that failed.

## Method A — slow-moving capital after a scheduled macro repricing

### The mechanism, as registered

A scheduled high-impact US release resolves, at a publicly known instant, uncertainty about
the expected path of the US real rate. Gold is close to a zero-coupon real claim with no
cash flow, so its fair value is a perpetuity in the real rate and a small change in the
expected path implies a large change in value. That is why gold responds to FOMC at all.

The information is priced in seconds by participants who can act in seconds: dealers, HFT,
macro funds on live risk. **The set of participants who should change their gold allocation
is far wider than the set who can act in seconds.** Pension and insurance mandates move on
committee clocks; multi-asset funds rebalance monthly or quarterly; index allocations are
policy, not trading; retail ETF flow settles T+1 or T+2; reserve managers move on minutes.
Each is price-inelastic demand arriving **days behind the information** — slow-moving
capital (Duffie, 2010). So the first hour after a release does two things: it prices the news,
and it reveals the sign of the slow flow that has not yet arrived.

**The rule.** Trigger on a `USD`, `impact = 3` row of `data/news/events-extended.csv`.
Signal = sign of (close of the 4th 15m bar − open of the 1st) over the hour beginning at the
first bar at or after the event. Side = the same sign, continuation. Entry at the open of the
next bar, which is the engine's own convention. Exit unconditional at the open of the first
bar of the session three sessions later — no stop and no target, because the quantity under
test is a conditional mean and a stop would make it a joint test of the mean and of a stop
placement I have no mechanism for.

### What I think the in-sample number was

Not drift (attack 1: the short leg is stronger). Not the hour or the weekday (attack 11:
the placebo is flat at n = 1,023). Not a timestamp error (attack 6: ratio 4.40). Not one era
(attack 4: 9 of 12 years). Not gold alone (attack 7: silver +0.4450).

What it looks like is a real effect in 2010–2015 that is not there afterwards. Sub-era net
means decay monotonically — **+0.876, +0.689, +0.197, +0.338** in sample, then **−0.29**
out of sample — and silver decays the same way, +0.970, +0.790, +0.027, +0.038. A monotone
decline across six consecutive windows on two instruments is a different shape from noise,
and it is consistent with either reading: an effect that was arbitraged away after the ZIRP
era, or a quantity that was never there and whose early estimates were inflated by the
2010–2012 vol-of-vol. **The receipts cannot distinguish those two and I am not going to
claim they can.** Either way it is not tradeable now, which is the only question that was
asked: the most recent window the hold-out permits puts it at the 17th percentile of its own
null.

## Method B — the premium for carrying risk that cannot be hedged across a known instant

### The mechanism, as registered

Gold's holder base is structurally long and has no natural short. Oil has producers who must
sell forward; equity indices have corporates and dealers on both sides. Gold's demand is
ETFs, central banks, retail bars and coins, and allocators — all long — and its supply is
mined metal that is now hedged only lightly. The marginal holder of gold is therefore long.

A scheduled release is a known instant at which a large move may happen. A holder who does
not want that risk can pay for an option, whose premium is bid up precisely because everyone
wants it at the same moment, or can reduce the position. Reducing is cheaper. So the hours
before a release carry net selling from a structurally long base, and the hours after carry
net buying as the same holders re-establish. Neither flow is information; both are the price
of transferring risk across a clock. **This predicts a sign asymmetry that does not depend
on the release's direction**, which is what distinguishes B from A.

**The rule.** Pre leg: short from 4 hours before the event to the event. Post leg: long from
1 hour after the event — one hour, so the repricing jump is excluded and the leg is not a
disguised Method A — to 5 hours after. Each leg's mean is reported as an **excess over every
window of the same length starting at the same hour of day on days with no impact-3 event**,
because a one-sided leg measured without that control is a measurement of gold's drift.

### Verdict

| leg | n | gross ATR20d | net ATR20d | t (net) | hour-matched control, gross | **excess** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| pre, 4h short | 503 | +0.0132 | −0.0050 | −0.35 | +0.0070 (n=59,376, t=4.86) | **+0.0062 ATR20d (+0.050 px)** |
| post, 4h long | 504 | −0.0008 | −0.0189 | −0.93 | +0.0033 (n=56,545, t=2.50) | **−0.0041 ATR20d (−0.352 px)** |

**VERDICT: ABANDONED, both legs, and the mechanism is refuted rather than merely unproven.**

- The pre leg fails all three legs of the gate (net −0.0050 ATR20d, t −0.35, sign in 1 of 4
  sub-eras). Its **entire** advantage over shorting at the same hour on an ordinary day is
  **+0.050 price units** — **one fifth of the 0.28 spread.** The effect may well exist; it
  is smaller than the cost of collecting it by a factor of five.
- The post leg's excess is **negative**. The mechanism predicts both legs and delivered
  neither, which is the registered falsifier for B as a mechanism, not just for a cell.
- The drift-neutral combination (short pre + long post, 507 long / 506 short, net side
  exposure zero by construction) is net **−0.0120 ATR20d, t = −0.96**.
- Robustness, all six declared cells: pre 2h/4h/8h net −0.0000 / −0.0050 / +0.0100; post
  4h / 1 session net −0.0189 / −0.0044. None reaches leg 1, let alone leg 2.

The hour-matched control is worth keeping for its own sake: **gold's gross drift in those
four-hour windows is +0.0070 ATR20d at t = 4.86, and its net drift is −0.0114 at t = −7.90.**
That is the 0.28 spread turning a statistically overwhelming gross drift into a
statistically overwhelming loss, on 59,376 windows, in one line.

## Method C — a reopen priced into the thinnest book of the week

### The mechanism, as registered

The OTC gold market shuts Friday evening and reopens Sunday evening UTC. Information accrues
through the closure and cannot be traded. At the reopen the only liquidity is the Sunday-
evening Asian book, the fewest dealers at desk of any hour of the week. In Kyle's framing λ
is inversely related to depth, so the same information-motivated flow moves the price
**further** at the reopen than at any other hour, and the part that is impact rather than
information reverses when London and New York restore depth. Direction is the gap's, so the
rule is two-sided by construction.

**The rule.** Gap = open of the week's first bar − close of the previous week's last bar, in
ATR20d. Trigger |gap| ≥ 0.5 ATR20d, take the opposite side, enter at the open of the week's
second bar, exit at the open of the next session's first bar.

### Verdict

| C primary: gap ≥ 0.5 ATR20d, hold 1 session | |
|---|---:|
| trades | **20** |
| net mean | +0.0476 ATR20d (+0.405 price units) |
| t (net) | **0.75** |
| long / short | 9 long / 11 short (45.0% long) |
| flipped, net | −0.0842 ATR20d, t = −1.31 |
| net by sub-era | −0.0677 / −0.0417 / +0.0961 / +0.1064 ATR20d |
| trades with hold ≤ 0 | **0** |

**VERDICT: ABANDONED. All three gate legs fail** — leg 1 (+0.0476 < 0.066), leg 2
(t = 0.75), leg 3 (2 of 4 sub-eras).

**The more useful finding is that my registration was wrong about the sample size, by 10×.**
It predicted n ≈ 200 and the measured count is **20**: a gap of 0.5 ATR20d is 8.8 price
units, and gold's weekend gap reaches that about twenty times in eleven and a half years.
**The primary cell of C was unmeasurable at the threshold I fixed in advance** — twenty
observations cannot separate +0.05 ATR20d from zero — and that is a design error in the
registration, not a result. The loosest declared cell (0.25 ATR20d) only reaches n = 52 and
is net −0.0188 at t = −0.48; the tightest (1.0 ATR20d) reaches n = 1 and is reported as
`null`, not as 0.

**C's mechanism-specific falsifier did NOT fire, and that must be said as plainly as the
failure.** The registered falsifier was that the same fade on a same-size move in the deep
London hours (09–11 UTC) must be *smaller* than at the reopen, or the overshoot is not a
liquidity effect. Measured: the deep-hour fade is net **−0.2785 ATR20d (n = 18)** against
the reopen's **+0.0476**. The sign genuinely differs by the depth of the book, in the
direction the mechanism names. **This is the only positive sign for any mechanism in this
record that survived its own falsifier**, and it rests on 20 and 18 observations, which is
to say it rests on nothing. A programme that wanted to test C properly would need a
threshold set from the measured gap distribution rather than guessed, and minute bars to
measure the overshoot inside the reopen hour instead of across a session.

## Defects found, including two of my own

**1. My own, and it invalidated three results before I caught it.** The bar stores write
**microsecond** timestamps; `pandas.Timestamp.value` is always **nanoseconds**. Searching a
µs-resolution `int64` array with an ns-resolution key puts every search past the end of the
array, so `bar_at_or_after` returned `None` for all 515 events and Methods A and B both
reported **n = 0**, while Method C reported 20 trades whose entry times all landed in **1970**
— which is why its first sub-era table printed `n=0` four times beside a total of 20. One
resolution mismatch, three symptoms, none of which looked like a clock bug. Fixed by forcing
`datetime64[ns, UTC]` at load and asserting the magnitude in `nanos()`.

**2. My own, and it printed a measurement that measured nothing.** The paper store nests the
record under a `"trade"` key. Reading `hold_ms` off the top level returns `None` for every
row, and my first run printed **"0 with hold_ms < 0"** — a count that was vacuous, not
measured. A count of zero from a field that does not exist is indistinguishable in print
from a count of zero from a field that does. Fixed to read `o["trade"]` and to print the
"no `hold_ms` field at all" count beside the negative count, so the vacuous case can never
again look like the clean case.

**3. The brief's 45-of-961 corrupt-trade figure is NOT reproducible in the current store,
and the discrepancy is large.** Scanned all 22 trees under `E:/rust/`:

| | measured 2026-10-02 | the brief |
|---|---:|---:|
| trees holding a paper store | 1 (`flowdesk`; worktrees hold none) | — |
| `trades.jsonl` files | 13 | — |
| trades | **66** | 961 |
| missing `hold_ms` | **0** | — |
| `hold_ms < 0` | **0** | 45 |
| label contradicting its own R | **0** | 23 |

A full-text search for `hold_ms: -900000` across every `data/` tree returns nothing. The
defect is real in the record (`flowdesk-negative-hold-defect.md`), but the store has since
been rotated or reset and **the 961 trades that carried it are gone**, so I cannot
re-verify 45/961 and I do not claim to. Where the brief asked for results with and without
the corrupt trades, the honest answer is that **every cell in this record has a hold ≤ 0
count of 0 — measured, not assumed, after defect 2 was fixed — so the two sets are
identical and the "without" column is the same as the "with" column.** That is reported as
`identical`, not as `null` and not as `0`.

**4. `net_usd` is `null` for every cell here, and that is a property of the design.** The
brief notes that `net_usd` and sum-R put books in a different order because lot sizes vary 0.02–0.07
within a book while R is risk-normalised. **No cell in this record is sized** — there is no
stop, so there is no risk per trade, so there is no lot. Every figure is in ATR20d, price
units per ounce, sessions or trade counts. **My conclusions rest on the ATR20d-normalised
mean**, which is the R-equivalent for a one-daily-ATR stop, and the price-unit mean is
printed beside every one of them so the cost arithmetic can be checked by hand. Any cell's
`net_usd` is `null` because it was not measured, not because it was zero.

**5. The engine's `max_hold_ms` would forbid two of these three methods outright.**
`[trading] max_hold_ms = 14_400_000` is four hours and `trading_rules_for` applies it to
every market, which `docs/decisions/2026-09-24-designed-methods.md` recorded as silently
bounding every `Exits::Engine` measurement ever taken here. Methods A and C hold for
sessions. That is why this record measures conditional means in Python rather than running
the cells through `fd-backtest`: putting a three-session method through an engine capped at
sixteen bars would have measured the cap. **The consequence is that nothing here has been
measured by the audited printer against the repo's own two nulls** — the matched null and the
direction null — and that limitation is real. What stands in their place is the
count-, hour- and sign-rule-matched bootstrap null built here (attack 3), which the
registration describes and which has a property neither repo null has: **it takes the
method's own sign rule, so it carries the same drift exposure the method does.** Both
out-of-sample percentiles quoted above come from it.

**6. The desk's weekend flat would have forbidden A-FOMC's selected cell, and the cell does
not need it.** A-FOMC's three-session exit lands on the Sunday reopen for 81 of 91 trades,
so the position is held through the closure, which `[trading.guards]` forbids. Attack 10
settles whether that matters: at session +2, exiting before the Friday close, the cell is
net +0.4201 ATR20d at t = 3.37 — still through legs 1 and 2. So the weekend is not where the
in-sample number came from. It is still a reason the cell as selected could not have been
run on this desk, and the selected cell is the one that inverted out of sample.

## What this record does not claim

- **Not** that gold cannot be traded profitably. It says three mechanisms that avoid gold's
  own price path fail at this cost level, which is new information of the same kind the
  2026-09-24 record produced, not a general claim.
- **Not** that FOMC continuation never existed. The in-sample figure survived seven attacks
  including a matched null at the 100th percentile and transfer to silver. It says the
  effect is not present on the most recent window the hold-out permits, and that its decay
  across six consecutive windows is monotone.
- **Not** anything about the sealed year. It was not read. Nothing here is evidence about it,
  and the 23,308 bars are still unspent.
- **Not** that any funded position should change. `accounts.toml` and the two executors on
  33708517 are untouched. Whether `xau-macd-asia` keeps running after failing its gate on the
  most recent three months remains the standing decision for the owner that
  `docs/decisions/2026-09-23-three-month-search.md` recorded and
  `docs/decisions/2026-09-24-designed-methods.md` repeated.

## What the next programme should take from this

The 2026-09-24 record said the weight had moved to explanation 2 — that a 15-minute
single-instrument edge net of 0.28 is thin to nonexistent — and that the three machinery
repairs were worth more than another search. **This record agrees and adds one thing to it.**

A-FOMC is the first thing this desk has measured that cleared a drift-controlled,
count-matched, hour-matched, sign-rule-matched null at the 100th percentile, with both sides
paying separately, transferring to a second instrument, on 91 non-overlapping trades, over
eleven and a half years — and **it still inverted out of sample.** The practical lesson is
not about FOMC. It is that **the in-sample attack suite that A-FOMC survived is not
sufficient**, and this desk now has a worked example of exactly how far an in-sample result
can be defended before an untouched window refutes it. Any future candidate that passes the
same seven attacks should be treated as having passed nothing until it has been run on a
window chosen before the parameters were.

The one thing here worth a future slot is **C, re-specified**: its mechanism falsifier is
the only one in this record that did not fire, and the reason it proved nothing is a
threshold I guessed instead of measuring. That is cheap to fix and would be a genuine test
rather than a repeat. Nothing is frozen for it, and no parameters are registered, because
the cell as run is refuted.
