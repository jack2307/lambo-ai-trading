# The entry problem is not the price, it is the bar between the record and the account

**Written:** 2026-09-17
**Status:** a measurement and a scoping, now with all four steps built. It is
not a hypothesis and changes no verdict. It recommends AGAINST the change it
was commissioned to cost, and against the reading that replaced it.
**Scope:** how an intent becomes a fill in the paper book, and when the mirror
learns about it. The record-keeping changes (step 2, step 3) are on `main` and
change no behaviour. **2a is built but OFF**: the split is on branch
`entry-lag-2a` and the poller half is behind `--fill-on-open`, which defaults
to off, so deploying it changes nothing until somebody passes the flag. Turning
it on is the owner's, and is a separate decision from merging it.

## What was asked and what changed

The question was what it would cost to let an intent fill at a PRICE rather
than at the next bar's open, because every real entry lands at xx:00/15/30/45
with the crowd. 6c measured that and the answer is that it is not worth
anything: the boundary minute is 13-33% more volatile but the fill is only
0.135 points worse per trade, spread does not widen at the boundary at all
(0.220 either side, 8,967 samples), and a limit at open±X suffers textbook
adverse selection — net EV between -0.34 and +0.20 points across all sixteen
(X, N) combinations, which is zero with noise.

While explaining a different field, 6c wrote that "the book only reports an
open position once the bar has closed". That sentence is the finding. It is
true, it is verified below at file and line, and the thing it describes is
**twenty-four times larger than the effect the whole investigation was about**.

## The defect, verified

### (a) When the paper book fills an intent

One bar after the decision, at that bar's open, and only when a CLOSED bar is
posted.

- `crates/fd-backtest/src/paper.rs:224` — `advance()` step 1: "Fill whatever
  the previous bar decided, at this bar's open."
- `crates/fd-backtest/src/paper.rs:331` — `decide()`: "what the strategy said
  on the bar just advanced, to fill at the next bar's open."
- `crates/fd-backtest/src/engine.rs:595` — the single line that prices it:
  `let entry = apply_costs(bar.open, side, true, rules);`. Both the backtest
  loop (`engine.rs:459`) and the paper book (`paper.rs:253`) call this one
  function, which is why the two agree trade for trade.

A forming bar never fills anything. `POST /api/paper/tick` writes to
`AppState::live_bars` and nothing else (`crates/fd-api/src/paper.rs:1864`); the
module docstring says it is "never in a run, never on disk, and never read by
anything in this module's decision path".

### (b) What the poller posts, and when

Closed bars only, and a bar is only closed once a newer one exists.

- `py/live/mt5_bars.py:9-12` — closed bars go to `/api/paper/bar`, "that is the
  bot's whole input"; the forming bar goes to `/api/paper/tick`, "that is for
  the screen alone".
- `py/live/mt5_bars.py:31-32` — "A bar is closed when the terminal reports a
  newer bar after it, so the last element of `copy_rates_from_pos` is" dropped.

So the bar stamped `t` reaches the API at about `t+15m`, not before.

### (c) What the mirror mirrors

The book's POSITION, not its INTENT.

- `py/live/mt5_executor.py:1445` — `book_open = run.get("open")`.
- The status payload carries both: `open: Option<OpenDto>` and
  `pending: Option<PendingDto>` (`crates/fd-api/src/paper.rs:973-978`). The
  pending field's own doc says "An entry decided at the last close and waiting
  for the next open." The executor does not read it.
- Executor poll interval: 15 seconds (`py/live/mt5_executor.py:554`).

### The sequence that follows

For a book on 15m bars, writing `t` for a bar boundary:

| clock | what happens | price involved |
|---|---|---|
| `t+15:00` | bar `B_t` closes; poller posts it within seconds | — |
| `t+15:00` | `advance(B_t)` fills the PREVIOUS pending at `B_t.open` | price at `t` |
| `t+15:00` | `decide()` stores this bar's intent as pending | — |
| `t+30:00` | bar `B_{t+15}` closes and is posted | — |
| `t+30:00` | `advance(B_{t+15})` fills that pending at `B_{t+15}.open` | **price at `t+15`** |
| `t+30:0x` | executor polls, sees a position for the first time, sends market | **price at `t+30`** |

The record says the trade opened at the `t+15` price. The account opened it at
the `t+30` price. Both are called the entry. **Confirmed.**

This is the unit-carrying disease with a clock instead of a currency, and it
has the same signature as the three in `2026-09-17-unit-carrying.md`: the code
is not wrong — `advance` is doing exactly what a backtest must do — and the
record agrees with it, so nothing downstream can contradict it.

### How large — measured, and it is not what it looks like

Re-exported `data/bars/XAUUSD-15m.parquet` (read-only, `mt5_export.py`, now
covering to 2026-09-17 13:00Z) and computed the SIGNED cost of filling one bar
later, in the direction actually traded, over every live paper entry. Control
books (`*-coin`) are excluded: they take the same bar with the opposite side,
so including them cancels the effect by construction. One entry is dropped for
a degenerate risk unit (`entry == stop`, R meaningless).

| n = 30 | points | R |
|---|---|---|
| mean | **−0.915** (se 1.045, t = −0.88) | **−0.079** (se 0.078, t = −1.01) |
| median | +0.210 | +0.009 |
| sd | 5.725 | 0.421 |
| min / max | −10.70 / +18.59 | −0.884 / +0.956 |

Worse in 13 of 30 entries — 43%, not 100%.

**The lag is not a systematic drain, and the note should not be read as saying
it is.** The mean is indistinguishable from zero at this sample (t ≈ −1), the
median is slightly POSITIVE, and the account gets a better price nearly as
often as a worse one. Anyone arguing for 2a on the grounds that the desk is
bleeding a bar's worth of price on every entry is arguing from a number that
does not exist.

What the lag is, is **0.42 R of dispersion per entry that the record does not
show at all**. Two consequences, and they are the argument:

- The research programme measures the paper book and the account is what
  earns, and the edge being measured is not established. The desk's own
  per-trade result over 6c's 55 entries is **mean +0.127 R with sd 1.063 R, se
  0.143, t = +0.89** — a mean nobody has demonstrated, inside noise that
  already swamps it. The lag then adds 0.369 R of dispersion the record never
  showed, which is **a third again** of the noise it did show. That is the
  honest form, and it is sharper than "a few hundredths of an R", which I had
  written and which understated the edge and the problem at the same time
  (6c's correction).
- The per-entry price is the SMALL failure. The large one is the trade that
  never happens, and it does not appear anywhere in the table above, because a
  trade the account never took has no slippage.

  **What such a miss costs, done correctly.** The first version of this note
  said the 12:00 terra LONG cost +1.32 R, which is the BOOK's R and is not what
  the mirror could ever have earned: a mirror only earns the part after its own
  fill, so the right quantity is `book_r − drift at the moment it would have
  filled`. Caught by 6c, which had made the identical error that morning in a
  `corr(drift, r) = +0.539`; the refusal log settles it (risk 12.55 points,
  target 4366.8, book exit 4366.66 = +1.32 R):

  | time | would_fill | drift | a mirror filling there earns |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 12:15:08 | 4354.27 | +0.34 R | **+1.00 R** |
  | 12:15:23 | 4366.94 | +1.35 R | 0.00 R — already through the target |
  | 12:15:38 | 4363.87 | +1.10 R | +0.23 R |
  | 12:15:53 | 4364.34 | +1.14 R | +0.20 R |
  | 12:16:08 | 4364.49 | +1.15 R | +0.18 R |

  and from 12:16 the price never came back below the target. So: at first
  sighting the account forwent **about +1.00 R**, and fifteen seconds later
  that was zero and stayed zero. The 0.32 R between the book's +1.32 and the
  achievable +1.00 is the lag itself, which is the one part of this the fill
  model could recover.

For reference, the unsigned volatility that produces this dispersion — the raw
size of one bar of gold, `data/bars/XAUUSD-15m.parquet`, last 8,640 bars:
median |open(t+1) − open(t)| = 3.270 points, mean 4.525, p90 9.660 (6c gets
2.68 median / 3.77 mean off the `state.json` windows — same quantity, same
order), which is
28% of one R at the median stop of 11.69 and 24x 6c's 0.135-point boundary
effect. That number says how much room the lag has to move in. It does not say
the lag costs it, and the table above is what settles that.

Two honest qualifications, because this number can be over-read:

- It is UNSIGNED. A delay adds ±0.28R of noise to every entry; whether it costs
  anything on average depends on whether the book's edge lives inside that
  first bar. If it does, the account loses the edge entirely. If it does not,
  the account still carries the variance, which degrades a Sharpe built on
  fractions of an R.
- The desk's whole research programme measures the PAPER book, and the account
  is what earns. A record that diverges from the account by 0.28R per entry of
  noise means a measured paper edge does not transfer, whichever way the sign
  falls.

### A second defect found on the way: the AI books fill before their model answers

Measured from `data/paper/*/decisions.jsonl`, n=321 decisions: model latency
p50 8.5s, p90 21.6s, max 54s; a decision is posted **24.2s after the bar closed
(p50), 199s at p90**.

`ai_trader` posts that intent to `/api/paper/intent`, which calls `decide()`
(`crates/fd-api/src/paper.rs:2716`), so it becomes the pending that fills at
`B_{t+15}.open` — **the price at `t+15:00`, which is 24 seconds before the
model said anything**, and over three minutes before it at p90.

For a rule strategy this is correct and instantaneous. For a model with
latency it is look-ahead in the live record, and it is biased in the book's
favour: if the model has any skill, the price before it spoke is better in the
direction it chose than the price after. It makes the paper book flattering
by construction, and it is the same bar that (a) makes the account late.

## Option 1 — limit entry. Do not build it.

Files it would touch, since the question was asked: `Intent::Enter`
(`crates/fd-strategy/src/registry.rs:95`) gains `entry: Option<f64>` and
`valid_bars: Option<u32>`, both `#[serde(default)]` so existing `state.json`
files reload; the fill branch at `crates/fd-backtest/src/paper.rs:224` and the
pricing at `engine.rs:595` learn a second path; `fills.jsonl` gains
`fill_kind: market_open | limit | gap_open`.

The gap rule is already decided by precedent and should not be re-argued:
`check_exit` (`engine.rs:676-678` for the stop, `685-687` for the target)
fills at the LEVEL when the bar traded
through it and at the OPEN when the bar gapped past it. An entry must do the
same, for the same reason — a bar that opens beyond your price never offered
your price.

**It would not pay.** 6c's net EV is zero with noise, and the mechanism is
understood rather than merely measured: a resting limit fills exactly when
price keeps coming at you (fwd|filled −2.57 on longs) and misses exactly when
it runs (fwd|missed +1.72), a 4.29-point selection gap against a 2-point
improvement. Roughly 40 cents a month across five books is the prize, and the
cost is a second fill path through the one function the golden suite pins.

## What it would break, if built anyway

- **Backtest parity: safe, conditionally.** Absent `entry` must mean
  market-at-open on exactly today's code path, byte-identical. The goldens
  (`tests/golden/`, `crates/fd-backtest/tests/paper_parity.rs`,
  `parity.rs`, `timeline_parity.rs`) replay strategies that never set the
  field, so they stay green if and only if the default branch is the existing
  branch and not a re-derivation of it.
- **Research: it is a new hypothesis, not a patch.** Every registration to date
  assumed next-open fills. A limit variant changes the fill model the null was
  built against, so it needs its own pre-registration under the research loop
  and its own matched null. It cannot be folded into an existing receipt, and
  saying so is not a formality — the desk has already closed 25 registrations
  under the old fill model.
- **The mirror.** A limit book needs either a pending order at the broker or a
  price poll in the executor, and `already_taken` stops being keyed to
  anything bar-shaped. It is currently keyed to "any closed trade of this magic
  entered at or after the book's entry stamp" (`py/live/mt5_executor.py:932`),
  which survives a limit fill unchanged — that test does not assume a bar
  boundary, only an ordering. This is the one piece that does NOT break.

## Option 2 — close the lag. This is the one worth costing.

### 2a. Post the open as soon as it exists

The poller sends the new bar's open at `t+15:00` the moment the bar opens; the
book fills the pending at that price, then the closed bar arrives at `t+30` and
only decides.

- **Price: identical.** The fill is still `B_{t+15}.open`. That is the claim
  1c asked me to check and it holds — but only if the fill is priced from the
  same field, not from a tick.
- **Cost:** `advance()` currently fills and advances in one call and `step()`
  composes fill/advance/decide (`crates/fd-backtest/src/paper.rs:196-209`).
  Splitting the fill out means a new API route that fills the pending from an
  open price without advancing the window, state persisted between the two
  halves, and the closed-bar path made idempotent so it does not fill twice.
  Roughly **6-10 hours**: `crates/fd-backtest/src/paper.rs`,
  `crates/fd-api/src/paper.rs` (route, DTO, state), `py/live/mt5_bars.py`,
  plus tests for the restart-between-halves case.
- **Risk:** it puts a new mutation on the one function the parity goldens pin,
  and a book that crashes between fill and decide is a state nothing has seen
  before. On a funded account.

**BUILT, 2026-09-17, on branch `entry-lag-2a` and off by default.** The
estimate was 6-10 hours across those three files and that is what it took.
What the scoping did not anticipate, and what the work found:

- **The two halves disagreed on the sizing ATR, and only an equality test
  could have caught it.** `accept` pushes the arriving bar and then trims the
  window's head; the indicators are computed on that window, and an indicator
  with memory never entirely forgets its seed. So a fill at the open and the
  same fill at the close were sized from windows offset by one bar: `risk`
  123.62542319257773 against 123.62541914739995, moving `stop` and `target` by
  4e-6 of a point. Nothing to a trade, and the same entry getting two different
  stops depending on which half filled it — the exact shape of defect this desk
  spent the day cataloguing. The fix is to compute over the window the trim
  will leave. The lesson is the test: written as bit-equality rather than as a
  tolerance, which is the only reason it was visible.
- **Adjacency was the wrong ordering rule.** `time == last + bar_ms` looks
  right and refuses the first open of every session, because this instrument
  has a weekend and an hour a day with no bars. The rule that works is the one
  the closed-bar path already uses — strictly newer than the last bar — which
  survives gaps and still refuses the case that costs money: an open replayed
  after its own bar closed, which would fill the NEXT bar's decision at the
  previous bar's open. Raised by a5; one ordering rule for one operation.
- **Idempotency needed nothing.** `pending.take()` is the evidence, and a
  crash between the halves degrades to today's behaviour: the fill is not
  persisted, the pending is still there, the closed bar fills it at the same
  price.
- **The counterfactual had to be seeded in the new half too.** `accept` clones
  the shadow book before stepping so it never inherits an advised fill;
  filling at the open without doing the same would have cloned a shadow that
  already held the position, and the advisor's whole measurement would have
  become a comparison of a book with itself. Nothing would have failed.
- **Two signatures now say what they read.** `open_position` takes a bar time
  and a bar open; `Guards::calendar_refusal` takes two times. Neither ever read
  anything else, and at the moment a live book can first act on a bar its high,
  low and close do not exist — so a `Bar` in either place would have been three
  invented numbers on the path that opens a position against a funded account.

### 2b. Mirror the intent instead of the position

The executor reads `pending` — already on the status payload, no API change —
and sends when the decision is made rather than a bar later.

- **Cost:** ~2-4 hours, entirely in `py/live/mt5_executor.py`, which is 5a's.
- **And it is wrong as stated.** A pending intent is not a commitment. Guards
  and the advisor are evaluated AT FILL TIME, not at decision time
  (`crates/fd-backtest/src/paper.rs:232-251`: the advisor cut, the guard
  refusal, then `open_position` which can still refuse for `NoRisk` or the
  notional cap). So the executor would sometimes open a position the book then
  refuses, leaving the account long while the book is flat — and unwinding it
  crystallises a result the book never took, which is precisely the harm 5a's
  `drift` comment refuses to cause. Making 2b safe means evaluating the
  refusals at intent time, which is engine surgery, at which point it is 2a
  with extra steps.

### A separate defect the same day exposed, and it is not this one

The drift guard is symmetric on a one-sided quantity. On 2026-09-17 it refused
a FAVOURABLE price four times out of six — terra SHORT at 09:30 (drift −0.27),
ds LONG at 11:30 (−0.35, a better price, and the 33-minute wait for it cost 3.5
points), ds SHORT at 13:00 (−0.32 to −0.63, better, refused five minutes), and
it is the mechanism that turned the 12:00 terra LONG into a trade the account
never took at all.

The chain is: the lag moves the price by a bar's worth → the shape check reads
that as drift → the mirror refuses → the trade is late or lost. So the lag is
the CAUSE and the symmetric guard is what converts it into a loss. They are two
fixes and they belong to two people: the guard is in `py/live/mt5_executor.py`
and is 5a's (fixed in 728a4a4). **Do not confuse them.** Fixing the lag alone
leaves a guard that refuses good prices; fixing the guard alone leaves the
account entering a bar late.

**What this section must NOT say, and said in its first version.** It claimed
the guard fix was "the one that stops a +1.32 R trade being missed". That reads
as: refused entries are the profitable ones. 6c tested exactly that over 81,789
observations of 15m gold, 2022-2026, measuring the return from the MIRROR's
fill rather than the book's:

| | n | mean return |
|---|---|---|
| ADVERSE, drift > +0.25 R | 25,560 | +0.0926 R |
| kept, \|drift\| ≤ 0.25 R | 31,679 | +0.0992 R |
| FAVOURABLE, drift < −0.25 R | 24,550 | +0.0938 R |

Equal to within 0.007 R, flat in both regimes, and `corr(drift, after-return)`
is −0.003 overlapping and +0.047 non-overlapping with the sign flipping year to
year. **Refused entries are not specially profitable.**

**And a conditional effect the unconditional test cannot see.** On bare bars,
one-bar drift carries no information about the next four hours — that is the
table above. CONDITIONAL on a strategy signal having fired, favourable drift is
an early stop-out warning: 7 of 8 favourable joins in the live sample were book
stop-outs against a 38% base rate, p = 0.006. The mechanism is not subtle — for
a long, price falling in the bar after entry is the first leg of the move to
the stop. Both results are true and they are answers to different questions,
which is the thing to carry away: a null over all bars does not license a null
over the bars a strategy chose. The consequence, recorded where the rule lives
rather than here: the favourable-join rule does not deploy and becomes a flag,
default off. The 12:00 terra miss is
one draw, not a property of the guard, and a recommendation resting on it would
be read as evidence when it is an anecdote. The guard change stands on its own
merit — a symmetric limit on a one-sided quantity refuses better prices, which
is indefensible whatever the return distribution turns out to be — and not on
that trade.

One counter-anecdote of the same weight, from this desktop's own records: the
single refusal here that can be matched to its outcome was a book position that
LOST 0.527 R, where the mirror sitting out saved it a further 0.29 R. n=1, and
quoted only to show that a single draw argues in whichever direction it fell.

### Context on the instrument, from 6c

Two measurements that bound what any fill-model change can be worth, and one
correction 6c issued against its own earlier statement:

- **The daily bar hole moves with US daylight saving.** Exactly one hour a day
  has no bars: 21:00Z from the March changeover to the November one, 22:00Z the
  rest of the year, with March and November split between them. Measured over
  100,586 bars, 2022-06-16 to 2026-09-17. This is why a full trading day is 92
  fifteen-minute bars and not 96 (Mon-Thu 92, Fri 84, Sun 8). 6c's earlier
  "21:00-22:00Z has no bars" was a September fact stated as a year-round one.
- **Spread does not widen inside a bar.** First 60 seconds after a 15m
  boundary: n=597, median 0.220, mean 0.220. Rest of the bar: n=8,370, median
  0.220, mean 0.220. Difference 0.000, flat in every 30-second bucket. The
  "spreads widen when everyone enters at xx:00" premise is false for this
  instrument at this broker.
- **Spread does widen by HOUR**, around the break: 0.21-0.22 from 00Z to 19Z,
  then 0.26 at 20Z, 0.29 at 21Z, 0.26 at 22Z, 0.21 at 23Z. **Sample caveat,
  which 6c asked to be kept in the note: this is two days** (8,967 samples,
  2026-09-15 to 2026-09-17, every hour represented with 356-544 each). Every
  hour is covered and two days is still two days; treat the 20-22Z widening as
  what this desk measured on the days it measured, not as a stable fact.

### Recommendation

**Do not build 2a on the strength of the price. Build the record, fix the
guard, and let the owner decide 2a against a number that now exists.**

Steps 1 to 3 below are done or in flight as of this note; 2a is not started and
waits on the owner.

1. **DONE.** The bars were re-exported (read-only) and the signed cost measured
   over 30 live entries: −0.08 R mean, se 0.078, median +0.01 R. The headline
   the note was commissioned to test — that the desk bleeds a bar of price on
   every entry — is **not supported**. What replaced it is the dispersion
   figure and the missed trade.
2. **DONE.** `fills.jsonl` gains an `opened` line carrying `time` (the bar the
   fill is PRICED at) beside `learned_at` (the wall clock this process LEARNED
   it), and `OpenDto` carries `learned_at` on the status payload. Until now an
   entry appeared in `fills.jsonl` only when it CLOSED, so the one moment worth
   timestamping was the one moment the file did not record. No behaviour
   change. The UI's `opened_at` on the BROKER side is a third clock — the
   account's real fill — and the desk should say which of the three it is
   showing; field names sent to 5a, which owns `Desk.tsx`.
3. **DONE.** The decision timestamp recorded against the fill price it
   received, so the AI look-ahead is visible in the record from here on: the
   `intent` line carries `decided_at` and the `opened` line that fill produces
   carries the same stamp.

   **A requirement that follows, and it is not optional.** Anything that
   reports a book's performance must cite this number. 24 s of hindsight on a
   15-minute bar is small; 199 s at p90 is a fifth of the bar, it is biased in
   the book's favour, and a favourable bias nobody quotes becomes "the strategy
   edge" by default. Recording it and then not citing it would leave the desk
   exactly where the five false comments of 2026-09-17 left it — a number that
   is true, written down, and read by nobody at the moment it matters.
4. **2a is built and waits for the owner to TURN IT ON.** It was 6-10 hours of his engine on a live
   real-money desk with five books open, and the case for it is now the
   dispersion and the missed trade rather than a per-trade price drain. If it
   is approved, the first test written must be a restart BETWEEN the two halves
   of the split `advance()`, not the last.

## What none of this fixes

If the model is consulted once per closed 15m bar, a better fill improves the
PRICE and not the TIMING of the decision. "Why every fifteen minutes" is a
cadence question about the book's timeframe and is a **separate decision with
its own cost**: model calls scale linearly with cadence, and the owner's own
Opus figure was $31-43/month at 15m — so 5m is three times that before any
other effect, and it changes the strategy's timeframe, which invalidates every
registration in exactly the way the limit variant would.

It must not be sold as part of this. One fix is not two.
