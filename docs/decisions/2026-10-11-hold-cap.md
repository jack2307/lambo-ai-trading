## 0. One correction to the brief, before anything else (§8: the measurement wins)

The brief says `max_hold_ms = 14_400_000` is "a hardcoded constant with no CLI
flag". **Half of that is wrong.** There is no CLI flag — true — but there has
been a config override since 2026-09-24:

    [markets.<id>.trading] max_hold_ms      (Option<i64>, engine.rs:363)
    [trading] max_hold_ms = 14_400_000      (config/default.toml:99, the fallback)

`crates/fd-backtest/tests/max_hold_horizon.rs` and `tests/trading_rules.rs`
already pin both sides of it, and `search.rs:214` already prints a `max hold:`
header line naming which of the two the run resolved. So the horizon was
*statable* — it was never *sweepable*, because stating it meant editing a shared
config file. What this job adds is the per-run flag.

**And one defect that bounds the whole result, already measured in-tree**
(`max_hold_horizon.rs::a_no_target_entry_is_given_one`, defect 5 of
`2026-09-24-designed-methods.md`): under `Exits::Engine`,
`Intent::Enter { target: None }` means *derive one from `reward_risk`*, not
*none*. `reward_risk = 1.8` lives in the shared `[trading]` table with no
per-market override. So lifting the hold cap does **not** make a position
unbounded — it hands the bound to a 1.8 R target. A reading of "no gain at 72 h"
is therefore a statement about *this* engine's expressible horizon, not about
the market, and this record will say so either way.

## 1. Hypothesis — one sentence

On the `Exits::Engine` rows where the four-hour cap is actually binding, lifting
it raises **gross per exposed session measured in round-trip spreads**, because
the cap — not the market — is what forces those rows to re-enter.

Two things this table says that the brief did not:

* **`max_drawdown_pct > 100%` on 7 of 10 rows** — addendum 6 §IV: those books
  blew up and their PF must not be read. The three rows where the cap bites
  hardest are also the only three with a surviving book. That is a coincidence
  this record notes and does not explain.
* **A mean hold of 313.8 min under a 255-min cap is not an error.** `check_exit`
  tests `bar.time - entry_time > max_hold_ms` on **bar boundaries**, so a
  position held into a weekend gap is cut at the first bar after the gap — up to
  ~49 h later. The cap is a *floor on when the clock may cut*, not a ceiling on
  hold length, and any "72 h" arm here means "72 h plus one gap".

## 3. Family membership rule — declared before the numbers

A row is in the family only if, at 4 h in window W2, guards off:

    TIMEOUT >= 20% of its exits       (the cap is actually binding)
  AND max_drawdown_pct < 100%         (the book did not blow; addendum 6 §IV)

Family: **`orb` (rangeMinutes = 60), `keltner-break`, `far-stop-break`.** Three
rows. Everything else above is reported and cannot carry a result.

## 4. Declared cells — the count BEFORE the numbers

**Part 1, instrument (not gate cells).** Four controls, each of which must pass
or the measurement is not published:

1. **Default byte-identity.** A fixed set of runs captured with the pre-change
   binary; the post-change binary, **passed no flag**, must reproduce them
   **byte for byte**. Defaults include the `max hold:` header line, so a run
   that resolved a different number cannot pass silently.
2. **A deliberately failing probe first.** `search.rs` already holds a test that
   scans its own source for flag literals and demands each be declared in
   `ALWAYS` / `BY_MODE`. I will add the flag's *reading code* first, run the
   suite, and **assert that test FAILS**, then declare the flag. Without that
   step a passing suite proves nothing (addendum 10 §VII).
3. **`Exits::Strategy` invariance.** The flag can only touch `Exits::Engine`:
   `check_exit` returns `None` for `self_managed` before it ever reads
   `max_hold_ms` (`engine.rs:772-777` vs `:800`). Measured, not asserted: a
   self-managed row at two flag values must change **0 trades**.
4. **Where it can be set from, stated.** CLI only. `[run]` in a batch file is
   **INERT** for this flag exactly as `null_sides` is (addendum 8 §VI), and the
   config keys above remain the other two sources. The receipt header must name
   which one the run resolved.

**Part 2, measurement.** Hold values, declared exactly:
**4 h (14 400 000 ms) / 12 h (43 200 000) / 24 h (86 400 000) / 72 h
(259 200 000)**.

    arm A, swap = 0      (`config/`)      3 rows x 4 holds x 2 windows x 2 guard arms = 48
    arm B, swap = -0.83  (`config-swap/`) 3 rows x 4 holds x 2 windows x 2 guard arms = 48

Windows are the record's two real gold windows (addendum 9 §II), not the 2-year
one the 4.04% cost figure came from:

    W2  --from=2018-06-01 --to=2026-06-01

`config-swap/` is **already in-tree** (commit `74a49a9`, arm B of `agent/n5`): a
byte-identical copy of `config/` whose only change is the generic gold rate

the brief's warning that "`markets.xauduka` has `swap = 0.00/0.00` ⇒ your

## 5. The quantity this job turns on, defined before it is computed

Both read off each row's own receipt, nothing re-derived from another job's
numbers (§8):

    trips per exposed session   = 1440 / mean_hold_min
    gross_usd                   = net_usd + spread_paid_usd        (commission = 0)
    G_trip                      = gross_usd / spread_paid_usd
    G_session                   = G_trip x trips_per_exposed_session

`G_session` is gross USD per 24 h of being in the market, priced in round-trip
spreads. **This definition is mine and may not be `mould-target`'s**, so no
absolute number here is compared to that job's 8.7× / 1.901× / 4.444; only the
*trend across hold values inside this measurement* is read.

## 6. Falsifier — specific and firable

**F1 (the main one). FIRES** if, on every family row and in **both** windows,
`G_session` fails to rise by at least **20%** from 4 h to 72 h.

Conclusion when it fires, in these words: **the four-hour cap was never what was
binding.** `mould-target` measured `G` flat in hold on `Exits::Strategy`
(1/2/5/15/30 sessions → 0.073/1.258/1.236/1.218/0.738); F1 firing makes that the
second measurement, on the second exit kind, and the long-horizon axis closes.

**F2 (carry).** If `G_session` does rise on some cell, but that cell's
`expectancy_net` in arm B is at or below its 4 h `expectancy` in arm A, the
result is reported as **"an account perk, not a strategy"** — in those words.

**F3 (instrument).** If the default byte-identity control fails, **no
measurement is published at all** and this record says so. Rewriting every
receipt in `docs/decisions/` is not a side effect this job is allowed to have.

**F4 (tool-blind).** If the `Exits::Strategy` invariance control shows any trade
change, my claim that the flag touches only `Exits::Engine` is false; stop and
report rather than reinterpret.

## 7. How to read it

* **`PF_r` beside `PF_usd` on every row, units named.** Seven measurements of
  that gap have given three answers (addendum 10 §V) — this job measures its own
  and quotes nobody's ratio. Gate decisions are taken on `PF_r` and `E`.
* **`E` from the `Lbar` identity line at 4 decimals**, not the 3-digit
  `expectancy` column (addendum 8 §IV).
* **Drawdown in USD beside every profit number**; `_pct` is a floor;
  `_pct > 100%` means the book blew and its PF is not read.
* **`--exit-mix` on every run, and the row's own rule must have fired.**
  For these three rows the engine *is* the rule, so the reading is the reverse:
  as the cap rises, TIMEOUT must fall and STOP/TARGET must rise. A row that
  still shows majority TIMEOUT at 72 h was cut by a gap, not by its logic.
* **`sized_down_by_guard` read from the printed `guards:` line** (which formats
  the field, `hypotheses.rs:752`), never grepped out of engine strings.
* **Gate counted by hand: 40 trades, not the tool's `need 30`.** Below 40 is
  NO CONCLUSION, not a rejection.
* **Percentiles are not published** unless `count match` is in band and there is
  a strong reason (addendum 8 §VI); the gate legs carry the verdict.
* **A cell that only survives with guards off is reported as NOT TRADEABLE**, in
  those words (addendum 5 §D).
