# The cost axis: is it the spread, or is there nothing to pay for?

Registered 2026-10-06, before the first `search.exe` of this axis. Agent m1,
worktree `/e/rust/fd-a1`, branch `agent/m1`, binary
`/e/rust/fd-wt-crt/target/release/search.exe` (built 2026-10-04 15:13 from
`agent/crt`; carries the drift-control null).

## Why this axis and not another

Every method this desk has closed died somewhere near its cost line. CRT was
the clearest case: one round turn took **12.50–14.07% of R** at 1H, against a
gate that asks for **+0.050R**, so the spread alone was 2.5–2.8x the entire
edge the gate demands. That makes "cost" the standing excuse for every
negative result, and an excuse nobody has shot at.

The owner is the introducing broker on his own accounts, so part of the
spread comes back: `config/accounts.toml` records
`[rebate] share_of_spread = 0.45`. That makes the excuse concrete and
testable, because the binary has two flags that reprice a run without a
single line of code changing.

## Hypothesis

One sentence: **if cost is what is killing the gold-intraday mechanisms, then
removing cost must bring at least one of them through the gate — and if
removing cost entirely still brings nothing through, cost was never the
binding constraint and "the rebate will save it" is an excuse, not a plan.**

Prediction, stated before the run: **0 cells pass at `--spread=0`.** I expect
the falsifier below to fire against the *cost* story, not against the
methods. I am registering the prediction so that the opposite result — cells
passing at zero cost and dying at the real spread — is a result I cannot
quietly reframe.

## The two controls, and what they are NOT

1. **`--spread=<price units>`** reprices the round turn for one run.
   Configured for `xauusd` is **0.28** (`config/default.toml:365`), which was
   a SINGLE terminal read on 2026-09-12; the spread logger has since sampled
   XAUUSD.sc 2,315 times and puts the p50 at **0.220** with a maximum of
   0.260, so every receipt in `docs/` is charged above anything ever
   observed. Repricing is a recorded amendment, never a config edit, and
   `config/` in this worktree is not touched by this axis.

2. **`--rebate-share=<share>`** credits a share of the round-turn spread
   **beside the book**. Read from source before registering
   (`crates/fd-backtest/src/bin/search.rs:265–283`,
   `crates/fd-backtest/src/rebate.rs`):

   - The flag is read in **`--mode=rescore` ONLY**. In `--mode=hypotheses` it
     is parsed by nothing and **silently ignored**. A receipt that passes
     `--rebate-share=` to a hypotheses run is a receipt measuring the config
     default, whatever its command line says. This is written down here
     because it is a trap I nearly walked into.
   - `rescore` takes `--batch-file=<toml>`, not `--batch=<name>`. There is no
     way to ask it for the built-in `gold-intraday`. So the thirteen
     hypotheses are transcribed into
     `docs/hypotheses/2026-10-06-cost-axis.toml`, committed with this file,
     field for field out of
     `crates/fd-backtest/src/hypotheses.rs:137–168`. Labels are made unique
     (the built-in reuses `intraday` four times); `base`, `filters` and
     `overrides` are verbatim.
   - The credit **never touches the cost model**. `rules.spread` stays what
     it was and the net columns come from a credited copy of the trades, so
     the gross column of a rescore run reproduces a `--mode=hypotheses` run
     of the same rows. That is the module's pinned invariant, and it is also
     why arm B is not simply arm A relabelled: in arm A the lower cost is
     visible to the walk-forward's **in-sample parameter selection**; in arm
     B selection happens on gross and the credit lands afterwards. Net P&L
     per trade is near-identical, the selected parameters need not be.

   Arithmetic, from `rebate.rs`:
   `credit = share x spread x lots x contract_size`, **one whole round turn
   per trade** — the engine charges half the spread on each side and the two
   halves are one spread, so crediting each side would be twice the truth.

   So `--rebate-share=0.45` prices a cost of `0.28 x 0.55 = 0.154`, and
   `0.9` prices `0.028`. Those equivalences are *why* the two arms are run
   against each other rather than one standing in for both.

## Falsifier — specific, and it can fire

**Primary.** At `--spread=0` — zero cost, which is strictly better than any
rebate obtainable anywhere on earth — if **0 of 13 rows** meet the gate on
**both** windows, then the cost axis is dead and I say so: these mechanisms
have no edge for cost to consume, and lowering the fee cannot rescue them.
This is a negative conclusion, and it is the point: it closes a direction the
desk is still hoping in.

**Converse.** If rows pass at `--spread=0` and fail at the real spread, cost
IS binding, the falsifier has NOT fired, and I owe a **breakeven spread**:
the level at which the passing cell's profit factor touches exactly 1.200,
found by bisection, reported against the real post-rebate cost of
`0.28 x 0.55 = 0.154`.

**What would make me distrust my own run.** A row whose trade count is under
40, which under this desk's own measurement is not a reading
(`PF 1.753 / 14 trades` and `PF 0.682 / 178 trades` from the same rule on the
same window). Such a row is reported as **not measured**, never as a pass.

## How to read it — fixed now, not after

- **Gate, not adjustable:** `profit factor >= 1.200` **AND**
  `expectancy >= +0.050R`. This is `config/default.toml:85–88`
  (`min_trades = 30`, `min_profit_factor = 1.2`, `min_expectancy_r = 0.05`)
  and it is not edited by this axis.
- **Minimum sample for a conclusion: 40 out-of-sample trades.** Below that the
  cell is `not measured`.
- **Both windows.** Window A `2025-07-01 → 2025-10-01`, window B
  `2025-04-01 → 2025-07-01`. A pass on one window is nothing.
- **Percentiles are not the gate.** This desk's median null runs at
  **PF 0.867 — under 1** — so "95th percentile" means "lost less than a
  random entry at the same cost and count", not "made money". Every
  percentile quoted in the report carries its own `null p50` beside it.
- **Market:** `xauusd` at the default `15m`
  (`XAUUSD-15m.parquet`, confirmed present in
  `/e/rust/flowdesk/data/bars/`). `--data=/e/rust/flowdesk/data`, read-only.
  `data-sealed/` is not opened, not pointed at, not counted. The `news:`
  source is whatever the receipt's own `news:` line prints and is quoted from
  there, not assumed — no row in this batch carries a `news:` filter.
- **Hold ceiling:** `max_hold_ms = 14 400 000` (4 h) applies, with no
  per-market override. It has silently killed methods before, so if a row
  changes behaviour across the ladder I check `--exit-mix` before reading
  anything into it.

### The falsifier did NOT fire, and I owe the converse

At `--spread=0` on Window A, **two of thirteen** rows meet the gate with 40+
trades, and on Window B **one** does. So "zero cost brings nothing through"
is false, and the primary falsifier is NOT shot. The converse branch is
taken: cost is binding on at least one mechanism, and I owe a breakeven
spread.

That said, the headline reading is still mostly negative, and precisely so:

**Twelve of thirteen mechanisms fail the gate at zero cost on both windows.**
For those twelve there is no edge for any fee to consume, and "the rebate
will save it" is an excuse. `intraday/ema-cross` at zero cost runs
PF 0.754 / −0.147R; `intraday/rsi-reversion` PF 0.712 / −0.144R;
`asia/bb-fade` PF 0.794 / −0.114R. A fee of nought does not move those
anywhere near 1.200.

### The one cell that crosses on both windows

`compression/bb-fade` — bb-fade gated to weekdays, flat across 16:30–18:15,
and ATR14/ATR100 in [0, 0.8]. 54 out-of-sample trades on Window A, 75 on
Window B. Window A is the binding window throughout, because its stop is
narrower (1.500 ATR = 3.97 points against Window B's 6.18), so the same

The `% of R` column at 0.154 is the engine's own printed figure

**Breakeven spread, Window A: bracketed in [0.210, 0.220], interpolated
0.214 price units per round turn — 5.39% of R.** PF is the binding leg at
every level; expectancy still clears +0.050R at 0.28.

**The real post-rebate cost is 0.28 x 0.55 = 0.154, which is 5.39% − 3.88%
= 1.51% of R inside breakeven.** On the logger's measured spread (p50 0.220
over 2,315 samples) the post-rebate cost is 0.121 = 3.05% of R, further
inside. So on this one mechanism the answer is: **cost is the binding
constraint, and the rebate closes the gap with room to spare.** At the full
configured 0.28 with no rebate it fails Window A; with the 0.45 share it
passes both.

Arm B agrees with arm A to within rounding, which is the cross-check the
registration asked for: `--rebate-share=0.45` gives PF net **1.240 / 1.394**
against `--spread=0.154`'s **1.241 / 1.411**. The in-sample selection seeing
the lower cost (arm A) versus the credit landing after selection (arm B) is
worth 0.001 PF on Window A and 0.017 on Window B here.

1. **Its own side is a coin.** The direction null puts it at the **50th
   percentile on Window A and the 45th on Window B** — its result sits at the
   median of its own randomly-flipped sides. It passes the gate without
   showing that the side carries information.
2. **Its matched null is unmatched, so the 72% / 88% percentile is not a
   reading.** The receipt says so itself: count match 0.31 and 0.25 (the

   not "made money".
3. **54 trades.** Above the 40 this registration declared, but this desk has
   measured the same rule on the same window give PF 1.753 at 14 trades and

The 4-hour hold ceiling is **not** what is holding it up, which was worth

### Not measured, and why

1. **Window B's breakeven spread.** It does not break even anywhere in the
   measured range [0, 0.28] — PF is still 1.320 at the configured spread.
   Extrapolating the curve past the highest level I ran is not a measurement,
   so this is **null**, not a number.
2. **The spread conditional on the compression regime.** This is the most
   important gap and it cuts both ways. `compression/bb-fade` fires *only*
   when ATR14/ATR100 is at or below 0.8, and spread and volatility are not
   independent. The logger's 0.220 p50 is **unconditional** over 2,315
   samples; the number that would actually decide this row is the spread at
   the moments the signal fires, and it is not in the data I was given. A
   quiet-market spread is plausibly *tighter* than 0.220, which would widen
   the margin — but I did not measure it and will not claim it.
3. **Whether the rebate is a share of the spread at all.** `accounts.toml`
   writes down `per_lot = 11.0` as "NOT IMPLEMENTED, AND LEFT WRITTEN DOWN
   BECAUSE IT IS THE LIKELIER TRUTH", because an IB rebate is usually quoted
   per standard lot. A flat per-lot credit and a share of the spread are not
   one number in two hats: a share pays nothing when the spread is nothing, a
   flat fee pays the same in a quiet hour as a violent one, and at 0.28 they
   can be made to agree while at 0.21 they cannot. `--rebate-share=` can only
   sweep the share. So **arm B is measured under a model the config itself
   says is probably wrong**, and in the compression regime — quiet hours,
   narrow spreads — it is exactly the regime where the two models diverge
   most. Getting the owner's real per-lot figure would change this arm's
   numbers and is the single cheapest thing anyone could do next.
4. **A third window.** The brief asks for two and I ran two. One cell
   crossing two windows with 54 and 75 trades is a signal to go and look, not
   a result.
