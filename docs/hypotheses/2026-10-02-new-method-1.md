liquidity-hour effect both average out of an unconditional autocorrelation.

I am also choosing horizons where the cost term is not the binding constraint. Angle 3
measured the cost bar falling 21× from an intraday stop (14.0% of R) to a swing stop
(0.67% of R). Two of the three methods hold for sessions, not hours.

## The cost gate, derived rather than asserted

`[markets.xauusd] spread = 0.28` price units per ounce, sampled 2,315 times, charged
half on entry and half on exit: **0.28 price units round trip.**

The standing gate is expectancy ≥ 0.05 R. For a method whose stop is one daily ATR20,
R is one daily ATR20, so cost is 0.28 / ATR20d of R. Over the design window gold's
ATR20d averages roughly 18 price units, so cost ≈ 0.0156 R and net 0.05 R needs gross
**0.066 R ≈ 1.2 price units per trade.**

**The numeric gate, fixed now:**

1. gross mean move per trade ≥ **0.066 × ATR20d**, measured as the mean of
   (exit − entry) × side in units of the ATR20d at entry;
2. **t ≥ 3.0** on that mean, Newey–West is not required because the trades do not
   overlap by construction, but overlapping cells will say so;
3. sign of the mean holds in **≥ 3 of 4 disjoint sub-eras** of the design window.

I state in advance which leg I expect to bind: **leg 2 and leg 3.** Leg 1 is permissive —
1.2 price units is 6.6% of a daily ATR, and a three-session hold will clear it on noise
alone about half the time. A method that clears leg 1 and fails leg 2 has told me nothing.

## Method A — slow-moving capital after a scheduled macro repricing

### Mechanism

A scheduled high-impact US release resolves, at a publicly known instant, uncertainty
about the expected path of the US real interest rate. Gold is close to a zero-coupon
real claim with no cash flow: its fair value is a perpetuity in the real rate, so a small
change in the expected real path implies a large change in fair value. That is why gold
responds to FOMC, CPI, NFP and PCE at all.

The information is incorporated within seconds by the participants who can act in
seconds: dealers, high-frequency liquidity providers, macro funds with live risk
mandates. **But the set of participants who should change their gold allocation in
response to a real-rate repricing is far wider than the set who can act in seconds.**
Pension and insurance mandates move on committee clocks. Multi-asset funds rebalance on
monthly or quarterly schedules. Commodity index allocations are set by policy, not by
traders. Retail ETF flow arrives through brokers and settles T+1 or T+2. Central bank
reserve managers move on meeting minutes. Every one of those is price-inelastic demand
whose arrival is **days behind the information**, which is the slow-moving-capital
argument (Duffie, 2010): capital that should be at a reallocation is not there when it
happens, and the price path afterwards is the record of it arriving.

So the first hour after the release does two things. It prices the news for the fast
participants, and it reveals the **sign of the slow flow that has not yet arrived.**

### The rule

- **Trigger:** a row of `data/news/events-extended.csv` with `currency = USD` and
  `impact = 3`. Over the design window that is FOMC, US CPI, US Employment Situation
  (NFP) and US Personal Income and Outlays (PCE).
- **Release window:** the four consecutive 15m bars beginning with the first bar whose
  open is at or after the event timestamp.
- **Signal:** `sign(close of the 4th bar − open of the 1st bar)`. If zero, no trade.
- **Side:** the same sign. Continuation.
- **Entry:** the open of the next bar after the release window, which is the engine's own
  convention (signal on bar i fills at the open of bar i+1).
- **Exit:** the open of the first bar of the session three sessions after the entry
  session, where a session is a distinct UTC date present in the bar store. Exit is
  unconditional — no stop, no target — because the quantity under test is the
  conditional mean, and a stop would make the measurement a joint test of the mean and
  of a stop placement I have no mechanism for.
- Cost of 0.28 price units is subtracted from every trade.

### Primary cell and multiplicity, fixed now

**Primary: release window 1 hour, hold 3 sessions, all four US impact-3 series.**

Robustness cells, declared in advance and never primary: hold ∈ {1, 3, 5} sessions ×
release window ∈ {1 hour, 4 hours} × subset ∈ {all US, FOMC only} = **12 cells**. A
robustness cell may break the primary. It may never rescue it.

### Falsifiers

- The primary cell fails any of the three gate legs → **abandon A.**
- Flipping every side does not destroy the edge → **abandon A**, and say that the
  measurement was gold's drift.
- Mechanism diagnostic, not a hard falsifier because n per series is 100–200: the
  mechanism says the effect runs through a *rate* repricing, so FOMC should not be the
  weakest of the four series. If it is the weakest by a wide margin the mechanism is
  wrong even when the pooled number is good, and I will say so.

### Prediction

I expect A to fail leg 2. Three sessions of gold noise is roughly 1.7 ATR20d of standard
deviation; a mean of 0.066 ATR20d over ~700 events gives t ≈ 0.066 × √700 / 1.7 ≈ 1.0.
To reach t = 3.0 the mean has to be about 0.20 ATR20d, which is 3× the gate and a real
effect, not a thin one. If slow-moving capital is worth 0.2 of a daily range over three
days it would be visible to anybody who looked, and the base rate on this desk is zero
survivors from 186 tested cells. **I am registering A because the mechanism is sound and
has never been tested here — every method in the record is forbidden from trading news by
the guards' news flat — not because I expect it to pass.**

## Method B — the premium for carrying risk that cannot be hedged across a known instant

### Mechanism

Gold's holder base is structurally long and has no natural short. Oil has producers who
must sell forward; equity indices have corporates and dealers on both sides. Gold's
demand is ETFs, central banks, retail bars and coins, and allocators — all long — and
its supply is mined metal that is hedged only lightly and, since the 2000s, largely not
at all. The marginal holder of a gold position is therefore long.

A scheduled release is a known instant at which a large price change may occur. A holder
who does not want that risk has two choices: pay for an option, whose premium is bid up
precisely because everyone wants it at the same time, or reduce the position. Reducing is
cheaper. So in the hours before a scheduled high-impact release there is net selling from
the structurally long holder base, and in the hours after it, once the uncertainty is
resolved, there is net buying as the same holders re-establish. Neither flow is
information. Both are the price of transferring risk across a clock.

This predicts a **sign asymmetry that does not depend on the release's direction at all**,
which is what distinguishes it from Method A. A and B can both be true, and can be run
together, because A conditions on the release's sign and B does not.

### The rule

- **Trigger:** the same USD impact-3 rows.
- **Pre leg:** short gold from the open of the bar 4 hours before the event to the open
  of the bar at the event. Direction is fixed short, with no conditioning.
- **Post leg:** long gold from the open of the bar 1 hour after the event — one hour
  after, so the repricing jump itself is excluded and the leg is not a disguised
  Method A — to the open of the bar 5 hours after the event.
- **Drift control, which is the whole measurement:** the mean of each leg is reported as
  an **excess** over the mean return of every window of the same length, starting at the
  same hour of day, on days with no impact-3 event in the design window. A one-sided leg
  measured without that control is a measurement of gold's drift and nothing else.
- Cost of 0.28 price units is subtracted from every trade.

### Primary cell and multiplicity, fixed now

**Primary: pre leg 4 hours, post leg 4 hours starting 1 hour after the event.**

Robustness cells: pre ∈ {2, 4, 8} hours × post ∈ {4 hours, 1 session} = **6 cells**.
Primary is (4 h, 4 h).

### Falsifiers

- Either leg of the primary cell fails any gate leg, measured **on the excess** →
  abandon that leg. Both legs failing → **abandon B.**
- Each leg is one-sided by construction: the pre leg is 100% short, the post leg is 100%
  long. The flipped test for a one-sided leg is arithmetically its own negation, so it
  carries no information; **the hour-of-day-matched control is the drift test for B**, and
  I register that in place of the flip. I will also report the drift-neutral combination
  (short pre + long post, equal lengths), whose net side exposure is zero.
- If the post leg's excess is positive and the pre leg's is not negative, the mechanism as
  stated is wrong — it predicts both — and a surviving post leg alone must be read as
  "something happens after releases", not as this mechanism.

### Prediction

The post leg is the one at risk of being gold's drift in disguise; the hour-of-day control
exists for that. I expect both legs to fail leg 2 of the gate. 4 hours of gold noise is
about 0.5 ATR20d, so a 0.066 ATR20d mean over ~700 events gives t ≈ 3.5 — this is the one
cell where the permissive leg 1 and the binding leg 2 are close together, which is why B
is worth registering at all.

## Method C — a reopen priced into the thinnest book of the week

### Mechanism

The OTC gold market closes Friday evening and reopens Sunday evening UTC. Information
accrues through the closure and cannot be traded. At the reopen the only liquidity on
the book is the Sunday-evening Asian session: the fewest dealers at desk of any hour of
the week, and the widest quotes.

In Kyle's framing the price impact coefficient λ is inversely related to the depth of the
book. The same quantity of information-motivated order flow therefore moves the price
**further** at the reopen than it would at any other hour, and the part of the move that
is impact rather than information is reversed when London and New York bring depth back.
So the reopen gap should partially revert during Monday.

The direction of the trade is the gap's, so the rule is **two-sided by construction** and
cannot be gold's drift unless gaps are themselves systematically signed.

### The rule

- **Gap:** open of the first bar of the trading week − close of the last bar of the
  previous week, in units of ATR20d at the previous week's close.
- **Trigger:** |gap| ≥ 0.5 ATR20d.
- **Side:** opposite the gap. Reversion.
- **Entry:** the open of the second bar of the week — the gap is known at the close of the
  first bar, and the engine fills at the next open.
- **Exit:** the open of the first bar of the next session.
- Cost of 0.28 price units is subtracted from every trade.

### Primary cell and multiplicity, fixed now

**Primary: threshold 0.5 ATR20d, hold 1 session.**

Robustness cells: threshold ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1.0} ATR20d × hold ∈ {4 hours, 1 session} =
**6 cells**. Primary is (0.5, 1 session).

### Falsifiers

- The primary cell fails any gate leg → **abandon C.**
- Flipping every side does not destroy the edge → **abandon C.**
- C has a second, mechanism-specific falsifier that the other two do not: the mechanism
  says the overshoot is a *liquidity* effect, so it must be **larger at the reopen than at
  an arbitrary same-size move in a deep hour.** I will measure the same reversion rule on
  moves of the same size in the London hours. If the London figure is as large, the
  mechanism is wrong and whatever is there is not a liquidity effect. This is a hard
  falsifier for the mechanism, not for the number.

### Prediction

I expect C to fail leg 1 as well as leg 2, because the holding period is one session and
the gap filter removes almost all of the sample: roughly 800 weeks in the design window,
of which perhaps a quarter clear 0.5 ATR20d, so n ≈ 200 and t = 3.0 needs a mean of
about 0.12 ATR20d net.

## Decision rule

- A mechanism is **abandoned** when any registered falsifier fires, and the record names
  which one.
- A mechanism **survives the design window** only by clearing all three gate legs on its
  primary cell, with the flip (or, for B, the matched control) killing it.
- A survivor is then run on OOS 1 and OOS 2 at the **same parameters, no re-fit**, and is
  reported against the existing nulls. The primary criterion is OOS 2.
- A survivor of all three windows is **not** carried to the sealed hold-out. I stop and
  say so. That decision is the owner's.
