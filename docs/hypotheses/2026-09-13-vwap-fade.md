# 2026-09-13-vwap-fade: price stretched from the session VWAP is pulled back by the orders benchmarked to it

**Registered:** commit 4510e92 — before any run
**Status:** decided → docs/decisions/2026-09-13-vwap-fade.md (failed the primary; confirmation not opened)
**Batch file:** `docs/hypotheses/2026-09-13-vwap-fade.toml`

## Claim

Large orders in BTC are worked against the day's VWAP by execution desks and
ETF creation/redemption flow. When price closes more than 1.5 ATRs away from
the session VWAP, those orders lean against the move, and the next hours
bring price back toward the VWAP more often than not. A mean-reversion
mechanism with a named counterparty, on a market where the volume that
defines the benchmark is real (Binance).

## Falsifier

Gate and ≥ 95th percentile of random entries in the same hours on the
primary; survives the confirmation at the broker's cost; direction null
outside. Both windows.

## Base method

`NEW: vwap-fade` — first close beyond `stretchAtr` × ATR14 from the UTC-day
VWAP; target the VWAP, stop `stopAtr` × ATR further out; no re-entry while
still stretched. Grid `stretchAtr` × `stopAtr`.

## Data

- Primary: `btc:5m` Binance 2024-08 → 2026-09, real volume
- Confirmation: `btcusd:5m` Vantage 2025-09 → 2026-09, tick volume, $17
  spread — opened after the primary result is recorded
- Weekdays and weekends both (24/7 market); no flat window.
- Rows: all day; US hours only (`hours:0930-1600`, where the benchmarked
  flow is); a 24-hour session vs a New York day would need a clock-anchored
  VWAP the indicator does not have — the UTC day is used, stated.

## Sample needed

Stretches of 1.5 ATR happen several times a day: thousands of signals on
the primary. Sample is not the constraint; cost is.

## What each outcome means

- Survives both → record, paper-run proposal. Note for the volume question:
  a fade trades often at small stops — the cost per lot is the whole story.
- Survives primary only → the $17 spread; record says so.
- Fails primary → closed; VWAP reversion at 5m on BTC has no edge at $5.
