## Claim

Candle Range Theory, as practised: a higher-timeframe candle forms a range
(candle 1). The next candle of the same timeframe trades beyond one extreme of
that range and **closes back inside it** (candle 2). Price then travels toward
the **opposite** extreme of candle 1's range (candle 3). Entry is candle 2's
close back inside, stop beyond the sweep extreme, target the opposite extreme.

Why such a structure could exist, stated as a reason and not as a backtest: the
extremes of a completed higher-timeframe candle are where stop orders and
resting limit orders accumulate, because they are the two prices every chart of
that timeframe draws. A move that takes one extreme and then closes back inside
is, on that reading, an execution of those resting orders rather than a
continuation, and the inventory acquired doing it has to be worked off in the
other direction — toward the other side of the same range, which is the nearest
price at which the opposite pool of orders sits.

**What is already measured and must not be re-measured.**
`crates/fd-strategy/src/pdhl.rs` mode 0 ("fade the first touch that closes back
inside") is this mechanism at daily granularity with a fixed-multiple exit. It
was swept in the 21-mechanism three-month programme
(`docs/decisions/2026-09-23-three-month-search.md`): 161 cells, 25
out-of-sample tests, **0 survivors**, and `pdhl` did not reach the in-sample
top five. The sweep-and-close-back idea is therefore already refuted *at that
granularity, with that exit*. Two of CRT's defining features were not in that
test and are the entire subject of this registration:

1. **Which candle defines the range.** `pdhl` uses the previous New York day
   and nothing else. CRT is drawn on 1H and 4H as often as on 1D.
2. **Where the target is.** `pdhl` targets `riskReward × risk`, a constant
   multiple. CRT targets the **opposite extreme of candle 1**, a reward that is
   large when the sweep is deep and small when it is shallow, and therefore a
   different exit rather than a different parameter. This is the feature most
   likely to matter and the one the record has never charged.

## Falsifier

A cell is **refuted** when any one of these fires. All are declared now.

- **F1 — the gate.** Fewer than 30 trades, or profit factor < 1.200, or
  expectancy < +0.050 R, on **either** window. The gate is
  `min_trades 30 / min_profit_factor 1.2 / min_expectancy_r 0.05` from
  `config/default.toml` and **no threshold in this repository will be moved by
  this branch**.
- **F2 — the drift-controlled null, multiplicity-corrected.** The cell's
  profit factor below the **99.44th percentile** of its exposure-matched null
  (`--null-sides=exposure`, 300 seeds) on either window. 99.44 rather than 95
  because nine cells are declared: 1 − 0.05/9 = 0.99444, which demands
  p ≤ 0.00556 per cell. 300 sized seeds floor the resolvable p at 0.00332, so
  this threshold **is** resolvable, and in receipt terms it means at most one
  of 300 seeds may stand above the cell. **This is a harder bar than the
  code's 95th gate, not a softer one**; the code's gate is untouched and the
  raw percentile is reported beside the corrected verdict in every table.
- **F3 — the match checks.** The null's count match, cost match, gross
  time-in-market ratio outside 0.75–1.25, or its achieved signed share of time
  more than 0.05 from the method's. A row outside any band is printed
  unmatched and **cannot be quoted as a survivor whatever its percentile**.
- **F4 — the flipped sides.** The same rule with both sides reversed (short
  the low sweep, long the high sweep) reaching a *higher* percentile than the
  cell itself on either window, or the two percentiles summing to within 10
  points of 100. A percentile that is one minus its mirror's is a statement
  about gold's direction and not about the rule — the signature
  `docs/decisions/2026-10-02-drift-null.md` found in the recent-year receipts.
- **F5 — the target is the claim, and it has a control arm.** For each of the
  three range timeframes, the `rr15` cell is CRT's structure with `pdhl`'s
  constant 1.5R exit. If the opposite-extreme cell does not beat its own
  `rr15` sibling on both profit factor and expectancy on **both** windows,
  then CRT's distinguishing exit adds nothing and the variable target claim is
  refuted even if some cell passes F1–F4. This is the falsifier the
  registration expects to fire.
- **F6 — the one-sided null has no valid form.** If any cell's realised long
  share is 0.0 or 1.0, its matched null collapses to the method itself and the
  percentile must be printed `null` — not 0, not 100, not `[]`. CRT is
  two-sided by construction (a high sweep is short, a low sweep is long), so a
  one-sided row would be a defect in my implementation and is a falsifier of
  the measurement rather than of the claim.

## Base method

`NEW: crt`, in `crates/fd-strategy/src/crt.rs`. Stateless, like every strategy
in this registry: candle 1 and candle 2 are rebuilt from the closed bars on
every call and nothing at index > i is read.

Fixed by fiat, declared here, **not swept** — each of these is a fork CRT has
and I am closing it rather than paying for it:

- Candle 1 and candle 2 are consecutive completed buckets of the range
  timeframe, bucketed on absolute epoch time by `time.div_euclid(htf_ms)`, the
  idiom `crates/fd-strategy/src/ict.rs::nearest_htf_gap` already uses. **This
  aligns the 1D bucket to UTC midnight, which is not `pdhl`'s New York day**;
  that difference is recorded as one of the ways the two methods are not the
  same method.
- Candle 2 must trade **strictly beyond** one extreme of candle 1
  (`high > c1.high` or `low < c1.low`) and **close strictly back inside**
  (`close < c1.high`, `close > c1.low`). No minimum sweep depth in pips and no
  wick-only variant: a wick-only version is a different theory and is not
  tested here.
- If candle 2 sweeps **both** extremes, the setup is discarded. An outside
  candle is not a rejection of one side.
- Entry is the signal on the **last bar of candle 2's bucket**, which the
  engine fills at the open of the next bar — the engine convention in
  `crates/fd-backtest/src/engine.rs`. No lower-timeframe trigger, no limit
  order, no retrace entry. Those are three more forks and all three are closed.
- Stop beyond candle 2's sweep extreme plus `bufferPips = 3.0` at
  `pipSize = 0.1`, i.e. 0.30 price units — **`pdhl`'s own default**, so the
  comparison with `pdhl` differs on one axis fewer.
- `maxRiskAtr = 3.0`, `atrPeriod = 14` — again `pdhl`'s defaults. A setup whose
  risk exceeds 3 ATR(14) is skipped.
- One trade per candle-1 range. No re-entry.
- No session filter, no volatility filter, no weekday filter. Filters are
  forks; the batch files declare `filters = []`. The engine's standing guards
  (1% risk, daily cap, news flat, weekend flat, cooldown, notional ceiling)
  stay exactly as configured, as they were for every row of the three-month
  programme.

## The declared multiplicity, which is fixed at nine

Two axes, and only the two the record has not already paid for:

| axis | values | count |
|---|---|---:|
| the range candle's timeframe | 1H, 4H, 1D | 3 |
| the target | the opposite extreme of candle 1; the 50% midpoint of candle 1; a constant 1.5 × risk | 3 |

**9 cells. Read on 2 windows = 18 reads. Nine more rows are produced by the
flipped-sides diagnostic and cannot be survivors.** No cell will be added after
this commit; amendments to this file are appended and dated and cannot add a
cell. If none of the nine passes, the answer is that CRT does not pass, not
that a tenth cell is needed.

The arithmetic that makes 9 the number and not 20: the last programme on this
desk incurred **247 distinct (run, row) pairs against 6 declared**, and 51 of
247 (20.6%) sat at or above the 95th percentile of their own null where 12
would be expected if calibrated. Nine cells at a Bonferroni-corrected 99.44th
is the whole of what this registration will look at.

- 300 seeds per null, which floors the resolvable p at 1/301 = 0.00332 against

## What each outcome means

- **Fails Window A → closed.** The grid is not widened, the exit is not
  changed, no session gate is added, and no tenth cell is tried.
- **Passes Window A, fails Window B → closed**, and the record says the
  structure was in the older venue and not in the broker's own recent bars.
- **Passes both, but F5 fires** (the opposite-extreme cell does not beat its
  own 1.5R sibling) → **CRT is not distinguishable from `pdhl` mode 0 by its
  exit**, and whatever survived survived on the range candle's timeframe alone.
  That is a narrower and much less interesting claim and must be reported as
  such.
- **Passes both and F5 does not fire** → a decision record, an explicit
  statement of the multiplicity actually incurred, and nothing else. Three
  programmes on this desk have closed with zero survivors, so the base rate
  says a survivor is more likely a defect in my measurement than an edge; the
  last effort on this branch will go to attacking my own result and the
  attacks will be reported whether or not they succeed.

## Amendment, 2026-10-04, appended before any 300-seed figure was read

Two facts the first execution of the binary printed, both recorded here before
the measurement they affect, neither of them adding or removing a cell.

### 1. The engine imposes a four-hour maximum hold, and it is NOT changed

`[trading] max_hold_ms = 14_400_000` in `config/default.toml` — four hours,
with no per-market override for `xauusd` or `xauduka`. The registration above
did not mention it because I had not read the receipt line that prints it.

**It stays exactly where it is.** Raising it would be a threshold moved and an
undeclared fork, and the whole record — every row of the three-month
programme, the recent-year screen and both funded books — was measured under
it. But it must be read as a confound on one axis of this registration, and in
one direction only:

- `rangeTf = 4` (1H): candle 3 is one hour. Four hours is longer than the
  trade's own horizon, so the cap binds rarely. Unaffected.
- `rangeTf = 16` (4H): candle 3 is four hours and the cap is four hours. The
  cap binds at roughly the moment the structure says the move is over.
  Approximately matched.
- `rangeTf = 96` (1D): candle 3 is twenty-four hours and the cap closes the
  position after four. **The 1D cells are measured over a sixth of the horizon
  their own structure asks for.** A 1D cell that fails may be failing the cap
  rather than failing CRT, and this branch cannot tell those apart. It is
  recorded as a limitation of the measurement, not repaired, and the three 1D
  cells are reported with this stated beside them. The direction is the safe
  one: a truncated hold cannot manufacture a passing row, it can only destroy
  one.

### 2. `--guards` must be passed explicitly, and it will be

The first invocation printed `guards: off (every number unguarded)`. The
registration's base-method section says the standing guards "stay exactly as
configured, as they were for every row of the three-month programme", so every
run that produces a figure in the decision record passes `--guards`.

### 3. The run that discovered both, reported rather than discarded

That invocation was a plumbing check — does the binary in *this* tree name
`crt` rather than refusing it, does the batch file parse, does
`--null-sides=exposure` take. It ran Window A at **20 seeds with guards off**,
which resolves no percentile worth quoting, and its receipt is kept at
`docs/research/runs/2026-10-04-crt/00-plumbing-check-20-seeds-no-guards.txt`.
It is a look at Window A and it is counted as one. What it showed, so it is
not hidden: all nine cells failed the gate on profit factor and on expectancy,
the best profit factor anywhere in the nine being **0.663** (`crt/4h-opposite`,
1,173 trades), and every cell's own null median also losing money. Nothing in
it is quoted as a result and the 300-seed guarded run replaces it.
