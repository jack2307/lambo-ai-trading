# 2026-09-13-pdhl: yesterday's high and low are where the day's orders rest

**Registered:** 2026-09-13 02:30 — before any run
**Status:** registered
**Batch file:** `docs/hypotheses/2026-09-13-pdhl.toml`

## Claim

The previous New York day's high and low are visible to every participant
and carry resting stops and limits. Two readings, tested as separate rows:
in the thin Asian hours (19:00–02:00 New York) a touch that closes back
inside is absorbed and reverts (**fade**); in the New York morning
(08:00–12:00) a close through the level is the stops being taken and
continues (**break**).

## Falsifier

For each row: fails the gate or is below the 95th percentile of the null
gated to the same session; or fails on the confirmation window; or the
direction null (gated the same way) is inside. **Both windows must survive**
(rule adopted after the London-range pass).

## Base method

`NEW: pdhl`. Levels = high/low of the last completed New York day with bars.
Fade: bar trades through the level and closes back inside → enter against
it, stop beyond that bar's extreme + `bufferPips`. Break: close beyond the
level → enter with it, stop at the level ∓ buffer. Target `riskReward × risk`;
risk capped at `maxRiskAtr × ATR14`. First qualifying bar per level per day.
Grid: `riskReward` × `bufferPips` (9 cells). Sessions are batch filters.

## Sample needed

Fade rows: two levels a day, touched perhaps a third of days → ~250
opportunities in three years. Break rows similar. ≥ 60 walk-forward trades
per row.

## What each outcome means

- Survives primary AND confirmation AND direction nulls → record, paper-run
  proposal.
- Survives one window only → closed as a regime result.
- Fails primary → closed. No wider grid, no other sessions.
