# 2026-09-13-doji: a doji at the end of a move marks exhaustion, and the move reverses

**Registered:** commit b801751 — before any run
**Status:** decided → docs/decisions/2026-09-13-doji.md (inside the matched null; loses on 2018–22 and 2025–26)
**Batch file:** `docs/hypotheses/2026-09-13-doji.toml`

## Claim

Asked for by the owner. A bar that travels a full ATR and closes within 10%
of its open, after a directional move of at least 1.5 ATR over ten bars, is
the point where the side that drove the move stopped getting filled. Fading
that bar — stop beyond its far extreme, target 1.5× the risk — captures the
reversal more often than the spread costs.

## Falsifier

Gate and ≥ 95th percentile of random entries gated to the same hours on the
primary window; survives the confirmation; direction null outside (the
direction null is the whole question here: a doji that is *continuation*
would put the fade at the bottom). Both windows.

## Base method

`NEW: doji-reversal` — parameters `bodyMaxPct 0.10`, `minRangeAtr 1.0`,
`trendBars 10`, `trendAtr 1.5`, `bufferPips 3`, `riskReward 1.5`. Grid
`bodyMaxPct` × `riskReward`. First doji of a move only.

## Data

- Gold primary `xauduka:15m` 2022-06 → 2025-04-10; confirmation
  `xauusd:15m` 2025-04-11 → 2026-09-11. Rows: all hours (flat over the
  break), New York hours.
- BTC primary `btc:15m` 2024-08 → 2026-09; confirmation `btcusd:15m`.
- 15-minute bars because that is where the pattern is used; 5-minute dojis
  are noise by construction.

## Sample needed

A doji after a 1.5-ATR move is a few times a week: several hundred signals
on each primary.

## What each outcome means

- Survives both windows and the direction null → record; a candle pattern
  with an edge would be the first.
- Fails primary → closed; the doji is a bar shape, not a forecast.

## Amendment, 2026-09-19: the primary table above still prints SURVIVES, and the decision moved that row inside the null

**What this file says.** The primary table reads:

```
doji/strict             61   1.203   0.125    0.858    0.986  100%  SURVIVES
```

and the paragraph under it: *"The strict row passes every in-sample
condition, by a hair on the gate (PF 1.203, 61 trades)."*

**What the decision says.** `docs/decisions/2026-09-13-doji.md` reports the
same row against a null gated to the hours **and matched to the row's own
trade count**:

```
doji/strict             61   1.203   0.125    0.842    1.378   90%  gate pass, inside
```

and explains where the 100% came from: *"The first receipt, `in-sample.txt`
before the fix, showed `strict` at the 100th percentile against a null with
p95 0.986 — the *allday* row's null, byte-identical, a ~300-trade
distribution applied to a 61-trade row."* The p95 of 0.986 printed in the
table above is that borrowed null, which is why it is identical to the
`doji/allday` row's.

**Which is right: the decision.** The 90th is computed against a control with
the same trade count as the row it judges; the 100% is a 61-trade row read
against a ~300-trade distribution, which is tighter and therefore flatters
every thin row. The fix is in the runner
(`hypotheses.rs::matched_rate`), and the addendum to
`docs/decisions/2026-09-13-night-synthesis.md` generalises it: *"every
matched-null percentile in the records above was computed before the fix …
the percentile columns in those tables should be read as **upper bounds**."*
This row is the record's own illustration of the size of the effect —
100th to 90th on 61 trades.

**What does not change.** The PF, the expectancy and the trade count in the
table are the row's own numbers and are unaffected. The direction null of
**99th (p 0.008)** is unaffected — it is a permutation of the method's own
sides and never depended on the random-entry control. And the verdict is the
same either way: the confirmation replays at PF 0.782 on 25 trades and the
never-seen third window at PF 0.618 on 121 with direction 34th, so the row
would have closed on the confirmation whatever its in-sample percentile said.

The word SURVIVES stays in the table. It is what the run printed before the
control was fixed, and a reader who cannot see the wrong number cannot check
the correction.
